Overview

What is GST registration?

GST registration gives your business a 15-digit GSTIN. It is mandatory once annual turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods or ₹20 lakh for services (lower in some states), or if you sell inter-state or through most marketplaces. Approval usually takes 3–7 working days after Aadhaar authentication, and you can now apply for several states in one go.

GST registration enrols a supplier of goods or services under the Goods and Services Tax law and allots a unique 15-character Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN).

It is governed by Sections 22 to 30 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 and Rules 8 to 26 of the CGST Rules, 2017. Registration is PAN-based and state-specific, so a business needs a separate GSTIN in every state or union territory from which it makes taxable supplies.

Once registered you can legally collect GST, claim input tax credit (ITC) on GST paid on purchases, issue tax invoices and sell across state borders and on e-commerce marketplaces.

Eligibility

Who needs GST registration? Turnover limits for 2026

Under Section 22, you must apply within 30 days of your aggregate turnover (all-India, same PAN, including exempt and inter-state supplies, excluding GST) crossing the limit in a financial year.

Compulsory registration, whatever your turnover (Section 24)

  • Inter-state taxable supply of goods
  • Casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons
  • Persons liable to pay tax under reverse charge (RCM)
  • E-commerce operators, and persons required to deduct TDS or collect TCS under GST
  • Suppliers of goods through e-commerce operators (see relief below)
  • Input Service Distributors and agents supplying on behalf of others
  • Suppliers of online information and database access (OIDAR) services from outside India
Relief for small online sellers

Since 1 October 2023 (Notification 34/2023-Central Tax), suppliers of goods below the threshold can sell within their own state on marketplaces without registration, using a PAN-based enrolment number. Service providers below the threshold selling through e-commerce operators are also exempt.

Example · Goods

Priya runs a home bakery in Pune. Her sales for FY 2026-27 reach ₹38 lakh by February, all within Maharashtra, so the ₹40 lakh goods limit has not been crossed. In March she receives a ₹5 lakh corporate order from Bengaluru. That inter-state supply of goods triggers compulsory registration under Section 24 before she ships, whatever her turnover.

Example · Services

Arjun is a freelance UI designer in Jaipur billing ₹1.8 lakh a month (₹21.6 lakh a year). Services carry the ₹20 lakh limit, so he must register within 30 days of crossing it. Exports to a US client count towards aggregate turnover too, and with a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) he can export without paying IGST.

What’s new

Latest GST registration changes, 2025–26

Every change that affects a new registration, newest first. We update this timeline as notifications are issued.

  1. 1 October 2026

    Register in multiple states at once New

    GSTN’s Multistate Registration facility lets a normal taxpayer apply for several states or UTs under the same PAN in one go. You fill the Common Registration Information once under a Master TRN; the portal creates a state-wise TRN for each state with those details pre-filled. Each approved state still gets its own GSTIN.

  2. 8 September 2026

    Biometric Aadhaar for every new registration Court order

    A Delhi High Court interim order directs GST authorities nationwide not to grant new registrations without biometric Aadhaar authentication. The matter is still before the court, so we confirm the live process when you apply.

  3. 1 September 2026

    Contextual guidance in Form REG-01

    The GST portal now shows field-by-field help inside the registration form, reducing errors that lead to officer queries.

  4. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 and Invoice Management System

    The new Income-tax Act replaces the 1961 Act (section mapping below). On the GST side, the Invoice Management System (IMS) is now central to input tax credit: buyers accept or reject supplier invoices before they flow into GSTR-2B.

  5. 20 November 2025

    Bank account within 30 days, or suspension

    GSTN advisory enforcing Rule 10A: add a PAN-linked bank account within 30 days of registration or before your first GSTR-1/IFF.

  6. 1 November 2025

    Rule 14A: GSTIN in 3 working days

    Optional route for applicants whose output tax on B2B supplies is up to ₹2.5 lakh a month (Notification 18/2025-CT). You can later withdraw from it using Form REG-32.

  7. 22 September 2025

    GST 2.0 rate rationalisation

    Main slabs are now 5% and 18%, plus 40% for specified luxury and sin goods (56th GST Council). Choose HSN/SAC codes carefully.

  8. From July 2025 tax periods

    Locked GSTR-3B and a 3-year limit on returns

    Sales figures auto-filled in GSTR-3B can no longer be edited (correct them through GSTR-1A), and returns cannot be filed more than three years after their due date. Getting registration and the first returns right matters more than ever.

Statutory timelines (Rule 9, CGST Rules)

The officer must approve or raise a query within 7 working days after successful Aadhaar authentication. Without it, or if the application is flagged for physical verification, the limit is 30 days. If the officer does not act in time, registration is deemed approved.

Risk

Penalty for not registering under GST

  • Penalty, Section 122(1): ₹10,000 or the tax evaded, whichever is higher.
  • Interest, Section 50: 18% a year on tax not paid, from the date it was due.
  • Lost credit: no input tax credit for the unregistered period, and your buyers cannot claim ITC on your invoices.
  • Prosecution, Section 132: for serious evasion above the notified monetary limits.
Worked example

A trader in Indore crossed ₹40 lakh in October but registered only in March, missing GST of ₹1,20,000 on sales in between. Exposure: tax ₹1,20,000 + interest at 18% (about ₹9,000 for an average five-month delay) + a penalty of ₹1,20,000 (the tax evaded, being higher than ₹10,000). Registering on time would have avoided all of it.

GST × Income tax

GST registration and the Income-tax Act, 2025

The Income-tax Act, 2025 (assented 21 August 2025) replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026. GST rules are unchanged, but the income-tax provisions every GST-registered business deals with now have new numbers. The tax department matches GST turnover with ITR turnover, so both must reconcile.

Old vs new income-tax references for GST-registered businesses
Topic Income-tax Act, 1961 Income-tax Act, 2025
Period of incomePrevious year & assessment yearSingle “tax year”
Presumptive taxationSections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AESection 58
Tax auditSection 44ABSection 63
TDS on contractors, fees, e-commerce, purchase of goodsSections 194C, 194J, 194-O, 194QSection 393 (consolidated tables)
Transition note

Income of FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is still assessed under the 1961 Act; the 2025 Act applies from tax year 2026-27. Under presumptive taxation, business income is deemed at 8% of turnover (6% for digital receipts) under both Acts, and your GST returns are the turnover evidence.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is the turnover limit for GST registration in 2026?

Registration is mandatory once aggregate turnover in a financial year crosses ₹40 lakh for suppliers of only goods or ₹20 lakh for suppliers of services. The goods limit is ₹20 lakh in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Puducherry, Sikkim, Telangana, Tripura and Uttarakhand, and the services limit is ₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura.

How long does GST registration take?

Once Aadhaar authentication is complete, the officer must act within 7 working days, and eligible small B2B suppliers opting for Rule 14A get registration within 3 working days. If Aadhaar authentication is not done or the application is flagged for physical verification, it can take up to 30 days. Booking a biometric appointment at a GST Suvidha Kendra can add a few days.

Is biometric Aadhaar verification mandatory for GST registration?

Rule 8(4A) already required biometric Aadhaar authentication at a GST Suvidha Kendra for applicants flagged by the portal. On 8 September 2026 the Delhi High Court, by an interim order, directed GST authorities across India not to grant any new registration without biometric Aadhaar authentication. The matter is still before the court, so we confirm the current process when you apply.

What is Rule 14A GST registration?

Rule 14A of the CGST Rules, inserted by Notification 18/2025-Central Tax with effect from 1 November 2025, is an optional fast-track for applicants whose output tax liability on supplies to registered persons will not exceed ₹2.5 lakh per month. After Aadhaar authentication, registration is granted electronically within 3 working days. Only one such registration per PAN per state is allowed.

Can I apply for GST registration in more than one state at once?

Yes. Since 1 October 2026 the GST portal’s Multistate Registration facility lets normal taxpayers apply for several states or union territories under the same PAN together. You submit the common details once under a Master TRN, the portal creates a TRN for each state, and every approved state gets its own GSTIN.

Can I get GST registration without a shop or office?

Yes. You can register using your residential address as the principal place of business, supported by an electricity bill or property tax receipt and, if the property is not in your name, a consent letter or rent agreement from the owner.

Is GST registration mandatory for selling on Amazon or Flipkart?

Generally yes under Section 24 of the CGST Act. However, since 1 October 2023 small suppliers of goods below the threshold may sell within their own state through e-commerce operators without registration, subject to the conditions of Notification 34/2023-Central Tax, and service providers below the threshold selling through e-commerce operators are exempt.

Is a bank account required at the time of GST registration?

Under Rule 10A you must furnish bank account details within 30 days of grant of registration or before filing GSTR-1 or IFF, whichever is earlier; otherwise the registration can be suspended. Many applicants add it in the application itself.

What is the penalty for not taking GST registration?

Under Section 122(1) of the CGST Act, a person who is liable but fails to register faces a penalty of ₹10,000 or the amount of tax evaded, whichever is higher, plus 18% interest under Section 50 on the unpaid tax.

Can I register for GST voluntarily below the threshold?

Yes. Section 25(3) allows voluntary registration. It lets you claim input tax credit, issue tax invoices to B2B customers and sell inter-state or online, but you must then file all GST returns.

How is the 15-digit GSTIN structured?

Digits 1–2 are the state code, 3–12 are the PAN, 13 is the entity number for that PAN in the state, 14 is 'Z' by default and 15 is a check digit.

Does the Income-tax Act, 2025 change anything for GST-registered businesses?

GST law is unaffected, but from 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act. 'Previous year' and 'assessment year' become 'tax year', presumptive taxation under sections 44AD/44ADA/44AE moves to section 58, tax audit under section 44AB moves to section 63, and most TDS sections (194C, 194J, 194-O, 194Q) are consolidated in section 393. Your GST turnover should still reconcile with the turnover in your income-tax return.

Which returns do I file after GST registration?

Regular taxpayers file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B monthly, or quarterly under the QRMP scheme if turnover is up to ₹5 crore. GSTR-9 is mandatory only if aggregate turnover exceeds ₹2 crore. Composition taxpayers file CMP-08 quarterly and GSTR-4 annually.
