Overview

What is FDI filing?

FDI filing is reporting foreign investment in an Indian company or LLP to the Reserve Bank of India under FEMA, 1999, on the FIRMS portal. The main forms are FC-GPR within 30 days of issuing shares, FC-TRS within 60 days of a transfer, LLP-I for LLPs, and the yearly FLA return by 15 July.

Foreign money comes with reporting duties. Miss them and fees follow.

Each form is filed by the Indian company or the resident party, reviewed by the authorised dealer (AD) bank, and approved on FIRMS. The rules are in the FEMA (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, the reporting regulations, and the RBI Master Direction on Foreign Investment in India.

Example · Seed round from abroad

A Bengaluru startup receives ₹2 crore from a US fund on 5 November 2026. It must allot the shares by 4 January 2027. It allots on 10 December 2026, so FC-GPR is due by 9 January 2027, and the company files its first FLA return by 15 July 2027.

How to file

How to file FC-GPR

  1. Receive the money: through banking channels, with FIRC and investor KYC.
  2. Value the shares: issue price not below fair value.
  3. Allot within 60 days: and file PAS-3 with the ROC.
  4. File FC-GPR: on FIRMS within 30 days, with certificates.
  5. Bank approval: answer queries until it is approved.

Late filing has a cost

A late FDI report can usually be regularised within three years of the due date by paying a late submission fee worked out by the RBI. Beyond that, the contravention has to be compounded, which takes longer.

Late filing

Missed an FDI deadline?

  1. Work out the delay: from the due date to the filing date.
  2. Within three years: file the form and pay the late submission fee.
  3. Fee: a fixed amount plus a part of the investment, growing with the delay.
  4. Beyond three years: apply for compounding with the RBI.
  5. Prevent it: keep a FEMA calendar.

Routes

Automatic or government route

  • Automatic route: no prior approval; most sectors, up to their cap.
  • Government route: prior approval for certain sectors and above some caps.
  • Land-border countries: always the government route, including where the beneficial owner is from such a country.
  • Prohibited: a few sectors, such as lottery and gambling, allow no FDI.
Tax

How foreign investment is taxed

Dividends and capital gains of foreign investors are taxed in India, with tax deducted at source, and treaty relief may apply. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025. The angel tax on share premium was removed from FY 2024-25.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is FDI filing?

FDI filing is the reporting of foreign investment received by an Indian company or LLP to the Reserve Bank of India under FEMA. Reports are filed online on the RBI's FIRMS portal through the Single Master Form and are reviewed by the company's authorised dealer bank.

Which laws govern FDI reporting?

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, the FEMA (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, the FEMA (Mode of Payment and Reporting of Non-Debt Instruments) Regulations, 2019, and the RBI Master Direction on Foreign Investment in India.

What is the Entity Master on FIRMS?

Before filing any form, the Indian company or LLP registers a business user on the FIRMS portal and creates its Entity Master, which records its existing foreign investment. Forms such as FC-GPR are then filed under the Single Master Form.

When is Form FC-GPR due?

Within 30 days of issuing equity shares, compulsorily convertible preference shares or debentures, or other capital instruments to a person resident outside India.

How soon must shares be issued after money is received?

The company must issue the capital instruments within 60 days of receiving the money. If it cannot, it must refund the money to the investor within 15 days after those 60 days end.

When is Form FC-TRS due?

Within 60 days of the transfer of capital instruments between a resident and a non-resident, or of the receipt or remittance of funds, whichever is earlier. The resident transferor or transferee files it.

What is the FLA return?

The Annual Return on Foreign Liabilities and Assets. Indian companies and LLPs that have received foreign direct investment or made overseas investment file it on the RBI's FLAIR portal by 15 July each year, for the position as on 31 March.

What forms does an LLP file?

Form LLP-I within 30 days of receiving foreign capital contribution or acquiring profit shares, and Form LLP-II within 60 days of a disinvestment or transfer between a resident and a non-resident.

At what price can shares be issued to a foreign investor?

For an unlisted company, not below the fair value worked out under an internationally accepted pricing method and certified by a chartered accountant, a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practising cost accountant.

Does every investment come under the automatic route?

No. Most sectors allow FDI under the automatic route up to a cap, but some need government approval, and a few are prohibited. Investment from an entity of a country that shares a land border with India, or where the beneficial owner is from such a country, needs government approval.

What if an FDI form is filed late?

A delay can usually be regularised within three years of the due date by filing the form and paying the late submission fee worked out by the RBI, which grows with the amount and the delay. Beyond that, or for other contraventions, the matter goes to compounding.

How is foreign investment taxed?

Dividends and gains of foreign investors are taxed in India, with tax deducted at source, and relief may be available under a tax treaty. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025. The angel tax on share premium was removed from FY 2024-25.
