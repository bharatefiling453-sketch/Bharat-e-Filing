# Tax Audit Due Date Extended to 21 October 2026: What CBDT Circular 7/2026 Means for You

_By Bharat e-Filing Tax Desk · Fact-checked by REPLACE_CA_NAME, CA · Published 1 October 2026 · Updated 4 October 2026 · 8 min read_

![Tax audit due dates for AY 2026-27: audit report moved from 30 September to 21 October 2026, ITR for audit cases from 31 October to 21 November 2026](https://bharatefiling.com/wp-content/uploads/blog/tax-audit-due-date-extended-2026-cover-1200x675.jpg)

> **Quick answer:** CBDT Circular No. 07/2026, dated 28 September 2026, extends the tax audit report deadline for AY 2026-27 from 30 September to **21 October 2026**, and the ITR deadline for companies, audit cases and partners of audited firms from 31 October to **21 November 2026**. Transfer-pricing cases and non-audit taxpayers are not covered.

| Compliance                                 | Old date          | New date             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Tax audit report (Form 3CA/3CB + Form 3CD) | 30 September 2026 | **21 October 2026**  |
| ITR for audit cases                        | 31 October 2026   | **21 November 2026** |

## Key takeaways

- Audit report: **21 October 2026**. ITR for audit cases: **21 November 2026**.
- Covers companies, taxpayers whose accounts must be audited by law, and partners of audited firms.
- Not covered: transfer-pricing cases (30 November), salaried taxpayers (31 July) and non-audit business (31 August).
- Pay any balance tax by **31 October**: the extension may not stop section 234A interest.
- Missing the audit report can cost up to **₹1.5 lakh**; a late ITR loses loss carry-forward.

If you run a business or practice that needs a tax audit, the last week of September was stressful. Two days before the deadline, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) gave relief. This guide explains what changed, who benefits, what the extension does not fix, and how to use the extra 21 days well.

## What exactly did CBDT announce?

On 28 September 2026, CBDT issued **Circular No. 07/2026** under section 119 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. It extends the due date of the return of income for AY 2026-27 for one group of taxpayers: those at serial number 2 of the table in Explanation 2 to section 139(1). Under section 44AB, the tax audit report is due one month before that return due date, so the audit report date moved with it. The update is also listed on the Income Tax Department's [e-filing portal news page](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/latest-news).

| Compliance                                 | Earlier due date  | Revised due date     |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------- | -------------------- |
| Tax audit report (Form 3CA-3CD or 3CB-3CD) | 30 September 2026 | **21 October 2026**  |
| Income tax return for audit cases          | 31 October 2026   | **21 November 2026** |

## Who gets the extended due date?

The circular applies where transfer pricing (section 92E) does _not_ apply and the taxpayer is one of these:

- **A company**, whether or not it has income.
- **Any other taxpayer whose accounts must be audited** under the Income-tax Act (a tax audit under section 44AB) or under any other law. Examples are proprietors, partnership firms, LLPs, HUFs, co-operative societies and trusts that need an audit.
- **A partner of a firm whose accounts must be audited**, and the partner's spouse where section 5A applies (income split under the Portuguese Civil Code, as in Goa).

### Where you fit among the four ITR due dates

Many people still think there are only two ITR deadlines. The Finance Act, 2026 replaced Explanation 2 to section 139(1) with a four-row table from 1 March 2026, and it already applies to AY 2026-27. See all dates on our [ITR filing guide](https://bharatefiling.com/income-tax-return-filing/#due-dates).

![Infographic of the four ITR due dates for AY 2026-27: 31 July for others, 31 August for non-audit business, 21 November for audit cases (was 31 October), 30 November for transfer pricing](https://bharatefiling.com/wp-content/uploads/blog/itr-due-dates-ay-2026-27-infographic-1200x675.jpg)

| Who                                                                 | Due date in law  | After Circular 7/2026 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------- |
| Transfer pricing cases (section 92E), including their partners      | 30 November 2026 | No change             |
| Companies, audit cases, partners of audited firms                   | 31 October 2026  | **21 November 2026**  |
| Business or profession without audit, partners of non-audited firms | 31 August 2026   | No change (passed)    |
| Everyone else, including salaried people                            | 31 July 2026     | No change (passed)    |

### Am I covered? A quick check

1. **Must you file a transfer-pricing report (Form 3CEB)?**
   - **Yes:** not covered. Your ITR is due 30 November 2026 and Form 3CEB is due 31 October 2026.
   - **No:** go to question 2.
2. **Is the taxpayer a company, or must its accounts be audited under any law?**
   - **Yes:** covered. Audit report by 21 October, ITR by 21 November 2026.
   - **No:** go to question 3.
3. **Are you a partner in a firm whose accounts must be audited?**
   - **Yes:** covered for your own ITR. Deadline 21 November 2026.
   - **No:** this extension does not apply to you.

> **A point most guides miss:** what matters is whether an audit is legally _required_, not whether you chose one. A small business that had its books audited voluntarily does not move into the audit category, so its due date stays 31 August.

## What the extension does not change

An extended due date gives you more time to file. It does not change how tax and interest work.

- **Interest under sections 234B and 234C stays.** These are for shortfalls or delays in advance tax, which was due during FY 2025-26.
- **Section 234A may still apply from 31 October.** The circular extends the date administratively under section 119 and does not amend section 139(1). It is silent on 234A, so pay any balance self-assessment tax by 31 October 2026 to be safe.
- **Transfer-pricing deadlines stay.** Form 3CEB remains due on 31 October 2026 and the ITR on 30 November 2026.
- **Audit limits stay the same.** The extension does not change who needs an audit.
- **Other forms need their own check.** Some forms are linked to the section 139(1) due date and others have fixed dates. Confirm each one with your CA.

## Why is the 1961 Act still being used?

The Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force on 1 April 2026, so many taxpayers are surprised to see this circular cite the 1961 Act. Income earned between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026 belongs to the old law's period, and **section 536** of the new Act (repeal and savings) lets the 1961 Act keep governing it.

That is why the circular uses section 119 of the 1961 Act read with section 536 of the 2025 Act, and why "assessment year" and "previous year" still apply to this return. From FY 2026-27, the new Act's single "tax year" takes over. Our [section map from the 1961 Act to the 2025 Act](https://bharatefiling.com/income-tax-return-filing/#new-act) shows the new numbers.

## Who needs a tax audit for FY 2025-26?

A tax audit under section 44AB is a check of your books by a practising Chartered Accountant, reported in Form 3CA or 3CB along with Form 3CD. The main triggers are:

| Who                                    | Audit needed when                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business (general)                     | Turnover or gross receipts above ₹1 crore                                                     |
| Business with low cash dealings        | Limit rises to ₹10 crore if cash receipts and cash payments are each within 5% of totals      |
| Profession (doctor, lawyer, architect) | Gross receipts above ₹50 lakh                                                                 |
| Professional under section 44ADA       | Profit declared below 50% of receipts and income above the basic exemption limit              |
| Business that left section 44AD early  | Opted out within the five-year lock-in (section 44AD(4)) and income above the exemption limit |
| Sections 44AE, 44BB, 44BBB             | Profit declared lower than the presumptive amount                                             |

> **Good to know:** presumptive taxpayers are outside the general limits. Section 44AD is available up to ₹2 crore turnover (₹3 crore if cash receipts are within 5%). Section 44ADA is available up to ₹50 lakh of receipts (₹75 lakh if cash receipts are within 5%). F&O traders: see our [F&O audit rules](https://bharatefiling.com/income-tax-return-filing/#fno).

## What happens if you miss the new dates?

| Default                                       | Consequence                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audit report not furnished by 21 October 2026 | Penalty under section 271B of 0.5% of turnover or gross receipts, up to ₹1,50,000. Not levied if you show reasonable cause (section 273B).  |
| ITR filed after 21 November 2026              | Late fee under section 234F of ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh. A belated return is allowed only until 31 December 2026. |
| Tax unpaid on the due date                    | Interest of 1% a month under section 234A on the unpaid amount.                                                                             |
| Loss return filed late                        | Business and capital losses of the year cannot be carried forward (unabsorbed depreciation is an exception).                                |

> **The costliest mistake:** a firm with a loss this year can lose the right to set it off against future profits just because the ITR was filed one day late. Use our [late fee and interest calculator](https://bharatefiling.com/income-tax-return-filing/#late-calc) to see the cost.

## Your 7-step action plan for the extra time

Three weeks sounds like plenty, but an audit depends on suppliers, banks and your CA. Work in this order:

1. **Close the books for FY 2025-26 and freeze the trial balance.** _By 8 October._
2. **Match turnover with GST returns and tax credits with Form 26AS and AIS.** GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B turnover should agree with your books. See our [GST return filing guide](https://bharatefiling.com/gst-return-filing/). _By 10 October._
3. **Share supporting papers with your CA:** loan statements, fixed-asset bills, [TDS returns](https://bharatefiling.com/services/tds-return-filing/) and related-party payments. _By 12 October._
4. **Assign the audit form to your CA on the e-filing portal** so they can upload it. _By 12 October._
5. **Review the draft Form 3CD.** Check especially cash payments above limits, late PF/ESI deposits and delayed MSME payments (section 43B(h)). _By 16 October._
6. **Accept the uploaded report from your worklist.** The filing is complete only after you accept it. _By 19 October, leaving a buffer before the 21st._
7. **Pay balance tax, file the ITR and e-verify** within 30 days of filing. _Tax by 31 October; ITR well before 21 November._

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the new tax audit due date for AY 2026-27?

Under CBDT Circular No. 07/2026 dated 28 September 2026, the tax audit report for AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26) can be furnished up to 21 October 2026 instead of 30 September 2026.

### What is the new ITR due date for audit cases for AY 2026-27?

Companies, taxpayers whose accounts must be audited under any law, and partners of such firms can file their AY 2026-27 return up to 21 November 2026 instead of 31 October 2026, provided transfer pricing does not apply.

### Does the extension apply to salaried individuals?

No. Salaried and other non-business taxpayers had a due date of 31 July 2026, and non-audit business and professional taxpayers had 31 August 2026. Circular 7/2026 does not change those dates.

### Are transfer pricing cases filing Form 3CEB covered?

No. Where section 92E applies, the ITR due date stays 30 November 2026 and the Form 3CEB report stays due on 31 October 2026. Circular 7/2026 covers only serial number 2 of the due date table.

### Will I still pay interest if I file by 21 November 2026?

Interest under sections 234B and 234C for advance tax shortfalls is not affected. The circular extends the date under section 119 and does not amend section 139(1), so interest under section 234A on unpaid tax may still run from 31 October. Pay any balance tax by 31 October 2026.

### What if I miss the 21 November 2026 deadline?

You can still file a belated return up to 31 December 2026, unless assessment is completed earlier. A late fee under section 234F applies, and business and capital losses of the year cannot be carried forward.

### What is the penalty for not filing the tax audit report on time?

Section 271B allows a penalty of 0.5% of turnover or gross receipts, up to ₹1,50,000. It is not levied if you show a reasonable cause for the delay under section 273B.

### Does a voluntary audit qualify for the extended date?

No. The extension applies where an audit is required by law, under the Income-tax Act or another law such as the Companies Act or LLP Act. Getting books audited by choice does not move you into the audit category.

### Why does the circular refer to the Income-tax Act, 1961 when the new Act is in force?

The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, but income of FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is still governed by the 1961 Act through the savings provision in section 536 of the 2025 Act. The circular uses section 119 of the 1961 Act read with section 536.
