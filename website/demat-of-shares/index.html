Overview

What does demat of shares mean for a company?

Under Rule 9B, private companies other than small companies must issue shares only in demat form and help shareholders convert existing certificates. The company gets an ISIN through a registrar and NSDL and CDSL, shareholders convert through their depository participants, and the company files PAS-6 every half-year.

Paper certificates are on their way out for most companies. Demat makes transfers, fundraising and records simpler and safer.

It is governed by Section 29 of the Companies Act, 2013, Rules 9A and 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014 and the Depositories Act, 1996.

Shareholders

What each shareholder does

  • Open a demat account with a depository participant, in the same name and order as on the certificate.
  • Fill the demat request form and surrender the physical certificate.
  • Registrar verifies with the company’s records and confirms.
  • Shares are credited to the demat account; the certificate is cancelled.

Past the date? Act now (Rule 9B)

Once the compliance date passes, a covered company can issue securities only in demat form, and holders can’t transfer physical shares. A pending funding round or share sale can stall until the ISIN is in place.

PAS-6

Half-yearly reconciliation in PAS-6

  • What it shows: issued capital against shares held with NSDL, CDSL and in physical form.
  • When: within 60 days of 30 September and 31 March each year.
  • Who signs: certified by a practising CA or company secretary.
  • How many: one for each ISIN, if there are several classes.
Exemptions

Who is outside Rule 9B

  • Small companies: paid-up capital up to ₹10 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore, not a holding or subsidiary company.
  • Government companies.
  • Producer companies: until 31 March 2028.
  • Small today, bigger tomorrow: 18 months from the year end after you stop being small.

Tax

Demat and your tax records

Converting physical shares to demat isn’t a sale or transfer, so there’s no capital gains tax. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27, the original cost and holding period carry on for any later sale.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

Is demat of shares mandatory for private companies?

Yes, for private companies other than small companies and Government companies. Under Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014, they must issue securities only in demat form and help shareholders dematerialise existing holdings. The compliance date for companies that were not small on 31 March 2023 was extended to 30 June 2025.

Which companies are exempt from Rule 9B?

Small companies and Government companies are exempt. Producer companies have until 31 March 2028. From 1 December 2025, a private company with paid-up capital up to ₹10 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore is a small company, unless it is a holding or subsidiary company or falls in another excluded class.

What if my company stops being a small company later?

A private company that ceases to be a small company gets 18 months from the end of the financial year in which it ceased to be small to comply with the demat requirements.

Are unlisted public companies also covered?

Yes. Unlisted public companies have been required to issue securities only in demat form and facilitate dematerialisation since 2 October 2018, under Rule 9A.

What is an ISIN?

An International Securities Identification Number is a unique code given to each class of a company's securities. A company obtains it by entering tripartite agreements with a registrar and share transfer agent and each depository, NSDL and CDSL.

What is the process to dematerialise company shares?

The company appoints a registrar and share transfer agent, signs tripartite agreements with NSDL and CDSL, and obtains an ISIN. Each shareholder opens a demat account with a depository participant and submits a demat request with the physical certificate, which the registrar verifies before the shares are credited.

What is Form PAS-6?

PAS-6 is the half-yearly reconciliation of share capital audit report, filed within 60 days from the end of each half-year. It reconciles the issued capital with the shares held in NSDL, CDSL and physical form, and is certified by a practising CA or company secretary.

Can shareholders transfer physical shares after the deadline?

No. In a company covered by Rule 9B, a shareholder must get existing shares dematerialised before transferring them or subscribing to further securities.

Do shareholders need their own demat accounts?

Yes. Each shareholder opens a demat account with a depository participant, such as a bank or broker, in the same name and order as on the share certificate, with PAN.

What happens to the old share certificates?

Physical certificates are surrendered with the demat request. After the registrar confirms, they are cancelled and the shares are credited to the shareholder's demat account.

Does the company need to keep paying the depositories?

Yes. A company covered by the demat rules must pay the depository and registrar charges on time and maintain the security deposit the rules require. Our professional fee is separate from these charges.

Is dematerialisation a taxable transfer?

No. Converting physical shares into demat form does not change ownership, so there is no capital gains tax. The original cost and holding period continue, and a later sale is taxed under the Income-tax Act, 2025 as before.
