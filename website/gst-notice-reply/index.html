Overview

What is a GST notice?

A GST notice is a communication from a GST officer, issued on the GST portal, asking you to explain a mismatch, file a missing return, pay a shortfall or show cause against a proposed demand or cancellation. Each notice has a form number, cites a section and gives a reply date, usually 7 to 30 days.

Most notices start with a simple data mismatch between your returns, your suppliers’ returns or your books. Answered early and properly, most close without any demand.

Notices are issued under the CGST Act, 2017, mainly Section 46 (non-filing), Section 61 (scrutiny), Section 65 (audit), Sections 73, 74 and 74A (demands) and Section 29 (cancellation), with forms set by the CGST Rules. Ignoring one usually leads to an order passed without hearing you.

What’s new

Latest changes for GST notices

Changes that affect how notices are issued and answered, newest first.

  1. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 in force

    GST turnover is still compared with the turnover in your income-tax return and tax audit report, so mismatches can trigger questions under both laws.

  2. 2025–26

    GST Appellate Tribunal hearing appeals Now

    Second appeals against orders of the appellate authority now go to the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), with a pre-deposit of 10% of the disputed tax.

  3. FY 2024-25 onwards

    Section 74A replaces Sections 73 and 74 Law change

    One provision for fraud and non-fraud demands: notice within 42 months of the annual return due date, order within 12 months of the notice (extendable by 6 months), and no penalty in non-fraud cases if you pay within 60 days of the notice.

  4. October 2024

    Invoice Management System (IMS)

    Accepting or rejecting supplier invoices in IMS now shapes GSTR-2B, the base for DRC-01C and ITC scrutiny. Wrong IMS actions show up later as notices.

  5. 2022–23

    Automatic mismatch intimations

    Rule 88C (DRC-01B) and Rule 88D (DRC-01C) let the portal flag mismatches automatically and block GSTR-1 if you don’t reply in 7 days.

Your right to be heard (Section 75(4), CGST Act)

An opportunity of hearing must be given when you ask for it in writing or when an adverse decision is contemplated. Always ask for a personal hearing in a show cause reply.

Risk

What happens if you don’t reply

  • Ex parte order: the officer decides on the information available, usually with tax, interest and penalty.
  • Penalty: in non-fraud cases 10% of the tax or ₹10,000, whichever is higher; in fraud cases up to 100% of the tax.
  • Interest: 18% a year under Section 50 on tax paid late.
  • Blocked filing: unanswered DRC-01B or DRC-01C intimations block your next GSTR-1.
  • Cancellation and recovery: an unanswered REG-17 can cancel your GSTIN, and unpaid demands can be recovered under Section 79, including from your bank account.

Income tax

GST notices and the Income-tax Act, 2025

GST and income tax look at the same business. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and turnover in GST returns is still compared with your ITR, AIS and tax audit report.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is a GST notice?

A GST notice is a written communication from a GST officer, issued on the GST portal, asking you to explain a mismatch, file a pending return, pay a tax shortfall or show cause why a demand, penalty or cancellation should not be made. Each notice names its form, the section of law and the date by which you must reply.

How do I check if a GST notice is genuine?

Every genuine notice appears on the GST portal under Services, User Services, View Notices and Orders, or in the Additional Notices and Orders tab. Communications from CBIC officers also carry a Document Identification Number (DIN) that you can verify on the CBIC website. Never pay through a link in an email or SMS.

What is the time limit to reply to a GST notice?

It depends on the form: 30 days for scrutiny notice ASMT-10, 7 days for DRC-01B and DRC-01C mismatch intimations, 7 working days for REG-17 and REG-03, 15 days to file the return after GSTR-3A, and the period stated in a show cause notice in DRC-01, usually 30 days. Officers can allow more time on request.

What happens if I do not reply to a GST notice?

The officer can pass an order ex parte on the information available, raising a demand with interest and penalty. Unanswered DRC-01B or DRC-01C intimations block your next GSTR-1, an unanswered REG-17 can lead to cancellation of registration, and unpaid demands can be recovered under Section 79, including by attaching bank accounts.

What is an ASMT-10 notice?

ASMT-10 is issued after scrutiny of your returns under Section 61 and Rule 99 when the officer finds discrepancies, for example between GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B. You reply in Form ASMT-11 within 30 days, either explaining the difference or paying any tax due with interest. If the explanation is accepted, the matter closes; otherwise proceedings under Section 73, 74 or 74A can follow.

What is a DRC-01 show cause notice?

DRC-01 is the summary of a show cause notice proposing a tax demand with interest and penalty under Section 73, 74 or 74A. You reply in Form DRC-06 with your submissions and documents, and you can ask for a personal hearing, which Section 75(4) requires when an adverse decision is contemplated.

What is Section 74A of the CGST Act?

Section 74A, inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, replaces Sections 73 and 74 for demands relating to FY 2024-25 onwards. A show cause notice can be issued within 42 months of the due date of the annual return, and the order is due within 12 months of the notice, extendable by up to 6 months. In non-fraud cases, paying the tax with interest within 60 days of the notice means no penalty.

What are DRC-01B and DRC-01C notices?

DRC-01B (Rule 88C) flags tax shown in GSTR-1 that is higher than tax paid in GSTR-3B, and DRC-01C (Rule 88D) flags input tax credit claimed in GSTR-3B that is higher than GSTR-2B. Within 7 days you must pay the difference through DRC-03 or explain it in Part B, otherwise your next GSTR-1 is blocked.

What is a REG-17 notice?

REG-17 is a show cause notice proposing cancellation of your GST registration, for example for not filing returns or not operating from the declared address. You reply in Form REG-18 within 7 working days. If cancellation is ordered, you can apply for revocation within the time allowed by Section 30.

Can I appeal against a GST order?

Yes. You can file a first appeal in Form APL-01 within 3 months of the order under Section 107, extendable by 1 month for sufficient cause, after paying the admitted tax and a pre-deposit of 10% of the disputed tax. Further appeals lie to the GST Appellate Tribunal (GSTAT), which is now hearing appeals.

Can I avoid penalty by paying before the order?

Often, yes. In non-fraud cases, paying the tax and interest within 30 days of a Section 73 notice, or within 60 days of a Section 74A notice, closes the proceedings without penalty. In fraud cases, early payment reduces the penalty to 15% before the notice and 25% within 60 days of it.

Do GST notices affect my income-tax return?

They can. GST turnover is compared with the turnover in your income-tax return, AIS and tax audit report, so a mismatch can trigger questions under both laws. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies; tax audit under section 44AB moves to section 63.
