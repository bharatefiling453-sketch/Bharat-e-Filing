Overview

What is a partnership firm?

A partnership firm is a business run by two to fifty people who agree to share its profits, governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. It needs a partnership deed and its own PAN, and is registered with the state’s Registrar of Firms. Registration is optional in most states, but an unregistered firm can’t sue to enforce its contracts.

The deed is the heart of the firm: it decides money, roles and what happens when someone leaves.

Partners are jointly and severally liable for the firm’s debts. If you want limited liability, compare it with an LLP before you start.

Example · Hardware store

Gupta Hardware in Indore is run by two partners sharing profits 60:40. The deed authorises 12% interest on capital and remuneration to both working partners, so these payments are deductible for the firm, with TDS deducted on them once they cross ₹20,000 a year.

How to register

How to register a firm, step by step

  1. Agree the terms: name, capital, profit share, pay and exit.
  2. Execute the deed: signed by all partners and stamped as your state requires.
  3. Get the firm PAN using the deed.
  4. File the section 58 statement with the Registrar of Firms, signed by all partners.
  5. Bank, GST and books: current account, GST if needed, and tax set-up.

Why register, even if it’s optional

Under section 69 of the Partnership Act, an unregistered firm can’t sue a third party to enforce a contract, and partners can’t sue the firm or each other to enforce their partnership rights. Registering keeps those doors open.

Tax

How a partnership firm is taxed

The firm is a separate taxpayer with its own PAN and return, taxed at a flat 30% plus surcharge and cess where applicable. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025; FY 2025-26 and earlier follow the 1961 Act.

Partner pay

Remuneration limits and TDS

  • Authorised by the deed: paid only to working partners.
  • Limit on book profit: first ₹6 lakh (or a loss): ₹3 lakh or 90%, whichever is more; the rest: 60%.
  • Interest on capital: up to 12% simple a year.
  • TDS: 10% when payments to a partner exceed ₹20,000 in a year, from 1 April 2025.

Changes

When the firm changes

Changes such as a new partner, a retirement, a new address or a new firm name are recorded with a supplementary deed and reported to the Registrar of Firms, as the Partnership Act and your state’s rules require.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is a partnership firm?

Under section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, a partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all. The persons are partners, and together they are the firm.

How many partners can a firm have?

At least two. The maximum is 50, under section 464 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with rule 10 of the Companies (Miscellaneous) Rules, 2014.

Is registration of a partnership firm compulsory?

Under the Indian Partnership Act, registration is optional in most states and can be done at any time, though some states have amended the Act for their firms. Registering is strongly advised because of the disabilities an unregistered firm faces under section 69.

What happens if a firm isn't registered?

Under section 69, an unregistered firm cannot sue a third party to enforce a right arising from a contract, and a partner cannot sue the firm or other partners to enforce rights under the partnership. The firm can still be sued by others.

How is a firm registered?

The partners file a statement under section 58 with the Registrar of Firms of the state where the firm's place of business is, with the partnership deed, address proof and prescribed details, signed by all partners. Once the Registrar is satisfied, the firm is entered in the Register of Firms.

What should a partnership deed contain?

The firm name and business, partners' names and addresses, capital contributions, profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital, remuneration to working partners, duties, admission and retirement of partners, and how disputes and dissolution are handled.

Does a partnership firm need its own PAN?

Yes. A firm is a separate taxpayer for income tax and needs its own PAN, which is also used for its bank account and GST registration.

How is a partnership firm taxed?

A firm is taxed on its own income at a flat rate, currently 30%, plus surcharge and cess where applicable. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025; earlier years follow the Income-tax Act, 1961.

Can partners be paid interest and salary?

Yes, if the deed authorises it. Interest on capital up to 12% simple a year and remuneration to working partners within limits based on book profit are deductible for the firm, and taxable for the partner as business income.

Is TDS deducted on payments to partners?

Yes. From 1 April 2025, a firm deducts tax at 10% on salary, remuneration, commission, bonus or interest paid to a partner when the total exceeds ₹20,000 in the year. The rule was section 194T of the 1961 Act and is carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Is a partner's share of profit taxed again?

No. A partner's share in the firm's profit, which has already been taxed in the firm's hands, is not taxed again in the partner's hands.

What are the partners' liabilities?

Partners are jointly and severally liable for the firm's debts, so each partner's personal assets can be used to pay them. Partners who want limited liability often choose an LLP instead.
