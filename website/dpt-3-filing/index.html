Overview

What is DPT-3 and who files it?

DPT-3 is a company’s annual return of deposits and of money received that is not treated as a deposit, such as loans from directors or other companies. It is filed with the Registrar by 30 June every year, with figures as on 31 March, by every company that has such amounts outstanding.

Most private companies borrow from their promoters or group companies. DPT-3 is how that money is shown to be lawful.

It is governed by Sections 73 to 76 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rules 2(1)(c) and 16 of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014.

“Sinha Agritech Private Limited” in Patna owes ₹25 lakh to its director and ₹40 lakh to a group company on 31 March 2027. Both are exempted, with the director’s declaration on file, and are reported in DPT-3 by 30 June 2027.

Deposit traps

When exempted money becomes a deposit

  • Customer advance not adjusted against supplies within 365 days.
  • Share application money not allotted in 60 days and not refunded in the next 15.
  • Director loan without the declaration that it isn’t from borrowed funds.
  • Loans from outsiders, such as friends who are not relatives, in a private company.

Declarations first, not later (Rule 2(1)(c)(viii))

A director’s loan is exempted only if the director declares, when giving it, that it isn’t from borrowed funds. Collect the declaration with every new loan, so the money stays exempted when DPT-3 is filed.

Who files

Who must file DPT-3

  • Private, public and one person companies with deposits or exempted receipts outstanding on 31 March.
  • Not covered: Government companies, banks, NBFCs and housing finance companies.
  • Nothing outstanding? There is nothing to report; we confirm from your balance sheet.
  • Also due: AOC-4 and MGT-7 every year. See Company Compliance.
Member deposits

If your company holds deposits from members

  • Private companies may accept deposits only from members, within Section 73 limits and conditions.
  • Return of deposits in DPT-3, with information audited by the company’s auditor.
  • Public deposits are not allowed for private companies.
Tax rules

Loans, DPT-3 and the Income-tax Act

The Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27, keeps the rule that loans and deposits of ₹20,000 or more must not be taken or repaid in cash, with a penalty equal to the amount. Interest paid on such loans may also need TDS.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is DPT-3?

DPT-3 is the annual return in which a company reports to the Registrar the deposits it holds and the money it has received that is not treated as a deposit, such as loans from directors or other companies. It is filed under Rule 16 of the Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014.

What is the due date for DPT-3?

DPT-3 is filed every year by 30 June, with figures as on 31 March of that year. For the financial year 2026-27, the return as on 31 March 2027 is due by 30 June 2027.

Which companies must file DPT-3?

Every company that has outstanding deposits, or outstanding money received that is not treated as a deposit, as on 31 March must file it. Government companies, banks, NBFCs and housing finance companies are outside these rules.

Does a company with no loans need to file DPT-3?

If the company has no deposits and no outstanding receipts that fall within the exempted categories as on 31 March, there is nothing to report and DPT-3 is generally not filed. We confirm this from your balance sheet.

Are loans from directors deposits?

No, if the director gives a written declaration at the time of giving the loan that the money is not out of borrowed funds. The amount is then exempted, but it is still reported in DPT-3.

Are loans from relatives of directors deposits?

For a private company, money received from a relative of a director is exempted, provided the relative declares that it is not given out of borrowed funds. It is reported in DPT-3.

When does a customer advance become a deposit?

An advance for the supply of goods or services is exempted only if it is adjusted against the supply within 365 days. If it is not adjusted within that time, it is treated as a deposit.

When does share application money become a deposit?

If shares are not allotted within 60 days of receiving the application money, and the money is not refunded within 15 days after that, the amount is treated as a deposit.

Does DPT-3 need an auditor's certificate?

A return of deposits must contain information duly audited by the company's auditor, so an auditor's certificate is attached where the company holds deposits. For exempted transactions alone, practice varies; we coordinate the certificate with your auditor where required.

Can a private company accept deposits?

A private company can accept deposits from its members within the limits and conditions of Section 73 of the Companies Act and the Deposit Rules. Deposits from the public are not allowed for private companies.

What is the penalty for not filing DPT-3?

Late filing attracts additional fees that rise with the delay. Under Rule 21 of the Deposit Rules, contraventions can also lead to penalties for the company and its officers in default.

Can loans to a company be taken in cash?

Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, loans and deposits of ₹20,000 or more should not be taken or repaid in cash; the penalty can equal the amount. Use banking channels for every loan you report in DPT-3.
