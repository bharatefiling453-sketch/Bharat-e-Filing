Overview

How are shares in a private company transferred?

The seller and buyer sign a share transfer deed in SH-4, which is stamped and lodged with the company with the share certificate within 60 days. The board approves it as the articles require, the register of members is updated, and the certificate is issued within one month. Demat shares move through the depository instead.

A share transfer moves ownership in your company. Done right, the records, the tax and the relationships all stay clean.

It is governed by Sections 44, 56 and 58 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014 and your company’s articles.

Example · Co-founder exit

A co-founder of “Bhatt Organics Private Limited” in Jaipur sells 20% of the shares to the other founder. The remaining shareholders waive their right of first refusal, SH-4 is signed on 3 November and lodged the same day, the board approves on 6 November, and the certificate is endorsed that week.

Demat

Physical or demat shares

  • Rule 9B: private companies other than small companies must issue and allow transfers only in demat form; the compliance date was extended to 30 June 2025.
  • Small companies are exempt, and producer companies have until 31 March 2028.
  • Demat transfers move through the depository participant, not SH-4.
  • Holding physical shares in a covered company? Dematerialise before transferring.

Check the price, not just the paperwork

For unlisted shares, a price below fair market value can create tax for both sides: the seller may be taxed on fair value, and the buyer on the shortfall above ₹50,000. A valuation report before signing avoids both.

Refusal

When the company refuses a transfer

  • Grounds must come from the articles, such as a right of first refusal.
  • Notice of refusal to the transferee within 30 days of receiving the deed.
  • Appeal to the Tribunal within 30 days of the notice, or 60 days if no notice was sent.
Transmission

Transmission after a shareholder’s death

  1. Nominee registered in SH-13? The nominee applies with the death certificate.
  2. No nomination? Legal heirs apply with a succession certificate, will or other proof.
  3. Board resolution registers the shares in the new name; no SH-4 is needed.
  4. Certificate endorsed or issued, and nominations updated.

Changing the board too? See Director Change.

Tax

Share transfers under the Income-tax Act, 2025

Capital gains on unlisted shares are reported in the seller’s return under the Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27. Gifts to relatives are not taxed in the recipient’s hands; other below-value transfers can be.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

How are shares of a private company transferred?

The seller and buyer sign a share transfer deed in Form SH-4, which is stamped and delivered to the company with the share certificate within 60 days of signing. The board approves the transfer as the articles require, the company updates its register of members, and the transfer is endorsed on the certificate or a new certificate is issued.

What is Form SH-4?

SH-4 is the securities transfer form prescribed under the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014. It records the transferor, transferee, number and distinctive numbers of shares, and the consideration, and is signed by both parties and a witness.

Within how many days must the transfer deed reach the company?

Under Section 56 of the Companies Act, 2013, the executed transfer deed must be delivered to the company within 60 days of the date of execution, along with the share certificate.

How soon must the company issue the certificate?

The company must deliver the certificate of transferred shares within one month from the date it receives the instrument of transfer, unless it is prohibited by law or an order.

Can a private company refuse a share transfer?

Yes, if its articles allow, for example under a right of first refusal or board approval clause. The company must send a notice of refusal within 30 days of receiving the transfer, and the transferee can appeal to the Tribunal.

Do private company shares have to be in demat form?

Private companies other than small companies must issue and allow transfer of securities only in demat form under Rule 9B of the Companies (Prospectus and Allotment of Securities) Rules, 2014, with the compliance date extended to 30 June 2025. Small companies are exempt, and producer companies have until 31 March 2028.

Is there a ROC form for a share transfer?

There is no separate ROC form for an ordinary share transfer in a private company. The change appears in the annual return MGT-7. If the transfer changes a significant beneficial owner, the company files BEN-2 within 30 days of receiving the declaration.

Is capital gains tax payable on a share transfer?

Yes, for the seller. Unlisted shares held for more than 24 months give long-term capital gains taxed at 12.5% without indexation, and those held for 24 months or less give short-term gains taxed at the seller's slab rate, under the rules carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Can shares be transferred below fair market value?

They can, but there are tax consequences. For unlisted shares, the seller's sale value can be taken as at least the fair market value, and the buyer can be taxed on the difference if the shares are bought below fair market value by more than ₹50,000. A valuation report helps.

What if the buyer or seller is a non-resident?

A transfer between a resident and a non-resident is reported in Form FC-TRS on the RBI FIRMS portal, generally within 60 days of receiving or remitting the consideration, and must follow the pricing rules under FEMA.

What happens to shares when a shareholder dies?

This is transmission, not transfer. The nominee, or the legal heir with a succession certificate or other proof, applies to the company, which registers the shares in their name. No SH-4 is needed.

What documents are needed for a share transfer?

The original share certificate, the signed and stamped SH-4, PAN and identity proof of both parties, the board resolution approving the transfer, any consent or waiver under the articles, and a valuation report where the price is not at fair market value.
