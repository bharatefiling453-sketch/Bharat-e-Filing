Overview

What is ADT-1 and when is it filed?

ADT-1 is the form a company files with the Registrar to report the appointment of its statutory auditor. It is due within 15 days of the meeting that appoints the auditor, whether the board appoints the first auditor or the members appoint one at the AGM for five years. It carries the auditor’s consent and eligibility certificate.

Every company needs an auditor from its first month. ADT-1 is how the Registrar knows who that auditor is.

It is governed by Sections 139 to 141 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rules 3 to 6 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014.

Eligibility

Who can be your auditor

  • A chartered accountant in practice, or a firm or LLP of chartered accountants.
  • Not an employee, officer or relative-linked person barred under Section 141(3).
  • Within the 20-company limit, excluding OPCs, dormant, small and smaller private companies.
  • Written consent and a certificate confirming the appointment meets these conditions.

File within 15 days (Rule 4)

ADT-1 is due 15 days from the meeting, not from the date you receive the minutes. After an AGM on 30 September, that is 15 October. Late filing attracts additional fees that rise with the delay.

Rotation

Which companies must rotate auditors

  • Listed companies.
  • Unlisted public companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more.
  • Private companies with paid-up capital of ₹50 crore or more.
  • Companies with public borrowings of ₹50 crore or more.

For these, an individual auditor can serve one five-year term and a firm two, then a five-year cooling-off period applies (Section 139(2)).

Auditor change

When the auditor changes mid-term

  • Resignation: the auditor files ADT-3 within 30 days, with reasons.
  • Casual vacancy: the board appoints within 30 days; members approve within three months if it followed a resignation.
  • Removal before the term ends: special resolution and Central Government approval in ADT-2.
  • New auditor: ADT-1 within 15 days of the appointment.
Tax audit

Statutory auditor and tax audit

The statutory audit under the Companies Act and the tax audit under the Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27, are separate. Your statutory auditor can do both, or another chartered accountant can do the tax audit. ADT-1 covers only the statutory auditor.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is ADT-1?

ADT-1 is the form in which a company informs the Registrar of Companies about the appointment of its statutory auditor. It is filed by the company under Section 139 of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule 4 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014.

When is ADT-1 due?

ADT-1 must be filed within 15 days of the meeting at which the auditor is appointed. For an auditor appointed at an AGM held on 30 September, ADT-1 is due by 15 October.

How long is an auditor appointed for?

At the first annual general meeting, the company appoints an auditor to hold office from the end of that meeting until the end of the sixth annual general meeting, a term of five years. Annual ratification by shareholders is no longer required since May 2018.

Who appoints the first auditor of a company?

The board appoints the first auditor within 30 days of incorporation. If the board does not, it informs the members, who appoint the auditor at an extraordinary general meeting within 90 days. The first auditor holds office until the end of the first annual general meeting.

Is ADT-1 needed for the first auditor?

The MCA form provides for reporting the first auditor, and filing it within 15 days of the board meeting is widely followed so the Registrar's record shows the auditor from the start. We recommend filing it.

What documents are attached to ADT-1?

The resolution appointing the auditor, the auditor's written consent, the auditor's certificate that the appointment satisfies the conditions of Section 141, and a copy of the company's intimation of appointment to the auditor.

What is a casual vacancy in the office of auditor?

A casual vacancy arises when the auditor's office falls vacant mid-term, for example on resignation. The board fills it within 30 days; if the vacancy arose from resignation, the appointment must also be approved by the members within three months. ADT-1 is filed for the new appointment.

What happens when an auditor resigns?

The auditor files ADT-3 with the Registrar within 30 days of resigning, stating the reasons. The company then fills the casual vacancy and files ADT-1 for the new auditor.

Can a company remove its auditor before the term ends?

Only with a special resolution of the members and prior approval of the Central Government, applied for in ADT-2 within 30 days of the board resolution, after giving the auditor a reasonable opportunity of being heard.

Which companies must rotate their auditors?

Listed companies, unlisted public companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more, private companies with paid-up capital of ₹50 crore or more, and companies with public borrowings of ₹50 crore or more. An individual auditor can serve one term of five years and a firm two terms, followed by a five-year cooling-off period.

How many companies can one auditor audit?

Under Section 141(3)(g), an auditor can hold audit appointments in up to 20 companies, not counting one person companies, dormant companies, small companies and private companies with paid-up capital below ₹100 crore.

Is the statutory auditor the same as the tax auditor?

Not necessarily. The tax audit under the Income-tax Act, 2025 can be done by the statutory auditor or by another chartered accountant. ADT-1 relates only to the statutory auditor appointed under the Companies Act.
