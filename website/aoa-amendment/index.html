Overview

How do you amend a company’s articles?

The articles of association are altered by a special resolution of the shareholders under Section 14. The board approves the draft and calls the meeting, the shareholders pass the resolution, and the company files MGT-14 with the altered articles within 30 days. Changes must not conflict with the Act or the memorandum.

The articles decide how your company is run day to day. When the business changes, the rules should change with it.

It is governed by Sections 5, 6 and 14 of the Companies Act, 2013, with the model articles in Table F of Schedule I.

Investor rights

What investors usually want in the articles

  • Pre-emption and right of first refusal on new shares and transfers.
  • Tag-along and drag-along rights on a sale.
  • Nominee director and observer rights.
  • Affirmative votes on key decisions.
  • Founder lock-in and transfer restrictions.
Good to know

Limits on what the articles can say

Three rules every change must follow.

  1. Section 6

    The Act wins

    Any clause that conflicts with the Companies Act is void to that extent.

  2. Memorandum

    Within the MOA Always

    The articles can’t go beyond the memorandum. Change the MOA first if needed.

  3. Private company

    Transfer restriction Required

    A private company’s articles must restrict share transfers and limit members to 200.

Put the deal in the articles (Section 14)

Investor rights that stay only in a shareholders’ agreement may not bind the company. Adopt them in the articles by special resolution before closing, so transfers, allotments and board seats follow the agreed terms.

Entrenchment

Locking key clauses with entrenchment

  • What it does: a clause can be changed only if stricter conditions than a special resolution are met.
  • Private company: added with the agreement of all members.
  • Public company: added by special resolution.
  • Notice: the Registrar is told in MGT-14 within 30 days.
Conversion

Changing between private and public

  1. Private to public: special resolution, private-company restrictions removed, at least three directors and seven members, and INC-27.
  2. Public to private: special resolution, Regional Director approval, then INC-27 with the order.
  3. Name: “Private” is added or removed, and a fresh certificate is issued.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What are the articles of association?

The articles of association, or AOA, are the company's internal rulebook. They cover share transfers, board and general meetings, directors' powers, borrowing, dividends and other management matters, within the limits of the Companies Act and the memorandum.

How do I amend the articles of association?

Hold a board meeting to approve the draft and call a general meeting, pass a special resolution of the shareholders under Section 14 of the Companies Act, 2013, and file MGT-14 with the Registrar within 30 days, with a copy of the altered articles.

What is a special resolution?

A special resolution is passed when the votes cast in favour are at least three times the votes cast against, at a general meeting called with notice stating the intention to propose it as a special resolution.

Can the board alter the articles?

No. Only the shareholders can alter the articles, by special resolution. The board approves the draft and calls the general meeting.

Why do startups amend their articles at funding?

Investor rights in a shareholders' agreement, such as pre-emption, right of first refusal, tag-along, drag-along, nominee directors and affirmative votes, are best written into the articles so that they bind the company and its shareholders. Courts have refused to enforce some private-company transfer restrictions that were not in the articles.

What is an entrenchment provision?

It is a clause in the articles that can be changed only if conditions stricter than a special resolution are met. In a private company it is added with the agreement of all members, and in a public company by special resolution. The Registrar is notified in MGT-14 within 30 days.

Should old articles be replaced with Table F?

Companies registered under the 1956 Act often have articles that refer to repealed sections. Adopting a fresh set based on Table F of Schedule I of the Companies Act, 2013, with your own changes, brings them up to date in one resolution.

Do the articles need to allow an increase in authorised capital?

Yes. Under Section 61, a company can increase its authorised capital by ordinary resolution only if its articles authorise it. If they do not, the articles are altered first by special resolution.

How does a private company convert into a public company?

It alters its articles by special resolution to remove the private company restrictions, increases its directors and members to the public company minimums, and files the resolution and altered documents with the Registrar, including INC-27.

How does a public company convert into a private company?

It passes a special resolution to alter its articles and applies to the Regional Director for approval. After the order, it files INC-27 with the Registrar within the prescribed time, and the Registrar issues a fresh certificate.

Can the articles say anything the company wants?

No. The articles cannot conflict with the Companies Act or the memorandum. Any clause that goes against the Act is void to that extent.

Does an AOA change affect income tax or GST?

Not directly. The PAN, TAN and GSTIN stay the same. But if the change alters who signs or who controls the company, update authorised signatories on the income-tax portal under the Income-tax Act, 2025, the GST portal and the bank.
