Overview

What is trust registration?

Trust registration is registering a trust deed, in which a settlor hands property to trustees for a purpose, with the Sub-Registrar under the Registration Act, 1908. It is compulsory for a trust of immovable property. A charitable trust then registers with the Income Tax Department so its income is exempt. Some states also require registration with the Charity Commissioner.

The simplest way to set up a charity in India.

A trust needs no members or board meetings, and it is run by trustees under the deed. Because the deed sets all the rules and is hard to change, drafting it well matters more than anything else.

Example · Village scholarship fund

Three siblings in Kochi set up “Amma Vidya Trust” with ₹1 lakh to fund scholarships. One sibling is the settlor and all three are trustees. They register the irrevocable deed with the Sub-Registrar, get the trust’s PAN, and apply for provisional tax registration before accepting donations.

How to register

How to register a charitable trust

  1. Plan: name, objects, settlor, trustees and initial property.
  2. Draft: an irrevocable deed with tax-ready clauses.
  3. Register: the settlor and two witnesses sign before the Sub-Registrar.
  4. PAN and bank: in the trust’s name.
  5. Tax registration: as a charity, plus donation approval.

Compliance

Yearly compliance for a trust

  1. Books of account: income, spending and donor records.
  2. Income application: at least 85% of income each year, or accumulated within the rules.
  3. Audit: audit report filed when the law requires it.
  4. Income tax return: every year, by the due date.
  5. State filings: accounts to the Charity Commissioner where applicable.

Need help with the return? See Trust and NGO ITR Filing.

Funding

Getting ready to receive funds

  • Donations: income tax registration, plus donation approval so donors can claim a deduction.
  • CSR funds: registration with the MCA in Form CSR-1.
  • Foreign funds: registration or prior permission under the FCRA, 2010.
  • Government grants: often need a NITI Aayog NGO Darpan ID.
Tax

How a charitable trust is taxed

A trust is not tax-exempt by default. Its income is exempt only once it is registered with the Income Tax Department and it applies its income to its objects. From tax year 2026-27, registration and exemption are under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which treats it as a registered non-profit organisation.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is a trust?

A trust is an arrangement in which a person, called the settlor or author, hands property to trustees to hold and use for the benefit of others or for a purpose. A charitable trust is set up for public purposes such as relief of the poor, education, medical relief, preservation of the environment or advancement of any other object of general public utility.

Which law governs a trust?

Private trusts are governed by the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. Public charitable and religious trusts are outside that Act and are governed by general law and, in some states, by a state public trusts law, such as the Maharashtra Public Trusts Act in Maharashtra, under which the trust registers with the Charity Commissioner.

Does a trust deed have to be registered?

A trust of immovable property must be created by a registered written instrument under section 5 of the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, read with the Registration Act, 1908. Even where it is not compulsory, registering the deed with the Sub-Registrar is strongly advised, because banks, donors and the Income Tax Department expect a registered deed.

How many trustees are needed?

There is no fixed minimum in central law, and a trust can have a single trustee. Most charitable trusts appoint at least two trustees so that decisions and bank operations are not held by one person. Some state laws and the trust deed itself can set their own rules.

Who can be a settlor or trustee?

Any person competent to contract can create a trust or act as a trustee, and the settlor can also be a trustee. Trustees must accept the trust, and they must act in the interest of the trust and its objects.

What should the trust deed contain?

The name and address of the trust, the settlor and trustees, the objects, the initial property, how trustees are appointed and removed, powers and duties of trustees, how accounts are kept, and what happens to the assets if the trust is dissolved, which for a charitable trust is a transfer to another charity with similar objects.

Can a charitable trust be revoked?

A charitable trust deed should be irrevocable. A revocable transfer can lead to the income being taxed in the settlor's hands and can block income tax registration as a charity.

Is a trust exempt from income tax?

Not automatically. A charitable trust must register with the Income Tax Department. For FY 2025-26 this is registration under section 12A or 12AB of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From tax year 2026-27 registration is under the Income-tax Act, 2025, as a registered non-profit organisation, with provisional registration for a new trust.

How much of its income must a trust spend?

To keep its income exempt, a registered trust generally needs to apply at least 85% of its income to its objects in the year, or accumulate it within the rules. This 85% condition continues under the Income-tax Act, 2025 for registered non-profit organisations.

Can donors get a tax deduction?

Only if the trust has approval for donations, known as 80G approval under the 1961 Act, with equivalent approval under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Donors then claim the deduction allowed by law.

Does a trust need to file returns?

Yes. A registered trust files its income tax return every year, gets its accounts audited when the law requires it, and files the audit report before the due date. Trusts in states with a public trusts law may also file yearly accounts with the Charity Commissioner.

Can a trust receive CSR or foreign funds?

To run CSR projects for companies, it registers with the MCA in Form CSR-1. To receive foreign contributions, it needs registration or prior permission under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010.
