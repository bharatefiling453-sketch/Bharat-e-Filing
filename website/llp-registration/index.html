Overview

What is a Limited Liability Partnership?

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a body corporate under the LLP Act, 2008, separate from its partners, whose liability is generally limited to their agreed contribution. It needs at least two partners and two designated partners, one resident in India, is incorporated through FiLLiP, and files its LLP agreement in Form 3 within 30 days.

The flexibility of a partnership, with the protection of a company.

There’s no maximum number of partners and no minimum capital. An LLP can’t issue shares, so businesses planning to raise equity usually choose a private limited company instead.

How to register

How to register an LLP, step by step

  1. DSCs: Class 3 digital signatures for the designated partners.
  2. Name: ending with “LLP”, reserved on the MCA portal.
  3. FiLLiP: with partner details, consents and office proof.
  4. Certificate of incorporation: with the LLPIN, PAN and TAN.
  5. Form 3: file the LLP agreement within 30 days.

Two filings a year, every year

Every LLP files Form 11 by 30 May and Form 8 by 30 October, even with no business. Each carries a late fee of ₹100 a day, with no upper limit, so missed years add up fast.

Compliance

Yearly compliance for an LLP

  1. Form 11: annual return, by 30 May.
  2. Form 8: statement of account and solvency, by 30 October.
  3. Audit: unless turnover is up to ₹40 lakh or contribution up to ₹25 lakh.
  4. DIR-3 KYC: for designated partners, as the rules require.
  5. Income tax: the LLP’s return every year, with TDS as applicable.

Tax

How an LLP is taxed

An LLP is taxed like a partnership firm, at a flat 30% plus surcharge and cess where applicable. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025. From 1 April 2025, TDS at 10% applies to payments to a partner above ₹20,000 a year.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is a Limited Liability Partnership?

A Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is a body corporate formed under the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008. It is a separate legal entity from its partners, and each partner's liability is generally limited to the contribution agreed in the LLP agreement.

How many partners does an LLP need?

At least two partners. There is no maximum number of partners. Partners can be individuals or body corporates.

What is a designated partner?

Every LLP must have at least two designated partners who are individuals, and at least one of them must be resident in India. Designated partners are responsible for the LLP's compliance with the Act.

Who counts as resident in India for an LLP?

For designated partners, a person resident in India is one who has stayed in India for at least 120 days during the financial year, as amended by the LLP (Amendment) Act, 2021.

How is an LLP incorporated?

Online on the MCA portal: reserve the name, then file the incorporation form, FiLLiP, with the partners' details, consent of the designated partners, and registered office proof. The certificate of incorporation is issued with the LLPIN.

When must the LLP agreement be filed?

The LLP agreement is filed with the Registrar in Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation, and any later change to it is also filed in Form 3 within 30 days.

What are the yearly filings for an LLP?

Form 11, the annual return, by 30 May, and Form 8, the statement of account and solvency, by 30 October, after each financial year. Each carries a late fee of ₹100 a day if filed late.

Does an LLP need an audit?

Yes, unless its turnover does not exceed ₹40 lakh in the financial year or its partners' contribution does not exceed ₹25 lakh, under rule 24 of the LLP Rules, 2009. Tax audit rules under the Income-tax Act apply separately.

How is an LLP taxed?

An LLP is taxed like a partnership firm, at a flat rate of 30% plus surcharge and cess where applicable. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Can partners be paid remuneration and interest?

Yes, if the LLP agreement authorises it. Interest on capital up to 12% a year and remuneration to working partners within limits based on book profit are deductible for the LLP, and from 1 April 2025 TDS at 10% applies when payments to a partner exceed ₹20,000 in a year.

Can a partnership firm or company convert into an LLP?

Yes. The LLP Act allows a partnership firm, a private company or an unlisted public company to convert into an LLP, subject to the conditions in the Act's schedules.

LLP or private limited company: which is better?

An LLP suits professional and family businesses that want limited liability with fewer formalities. A private limited company suits businesses that plan to raise equity from investors, since an LLP cannot issue shares.
