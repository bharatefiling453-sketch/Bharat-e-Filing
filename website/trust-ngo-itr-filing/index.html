Overview

What is trust and NGO tax filing?

Trusts, societies and Section 8 companies registered under Section 12AB file their return in ITR-7. Their income is exempt under Sections 11 and 12 if at least 85% is applied to their objects and the accounts are audited in Form 10B or 10BB. For FY 2025-26, audited trusts file by 21 November 2026.

The exemption isn’t automatic. It is earned every year through the right application, the right forms and a return filed on time.

Returns are filed under Section 139(4A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961. NGOs receiving foreign contribution also file Form FC-4 under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010.

Application of income

The 85% rule, accumulation and deemed application

  • Apply 85%: at least 85% of income must be spent on the trust’s objects in the year.
  • 15% free: up to 15% can be set aside with no conditions.
  • Form 10: accumulate more for a stated purpose for up to five years, invested in Section 11(5) modes.
  • Form 9A: treat income as applied when it was received late or not received in the year.
  • Unspent: anything left over becomes taxable at the maximum marginal rate.
Example · Education trust

A Varanasi education trust earns ₹40 lakh and spends ₹32 lakh on scholarships and school running costs, 80%. It must apply ₹34 lakh, so it files Form 10 to accumulate ₹2 lakh for a new classroom within five years. Nothing becomes taxable.

Donations & 80G

Donations, 80G and donor filings

  1. Corpus donations: exempt and outside the 85% test, if invested in Section 11(5) modes.
  2. Anonymous donations: taxed at 30% above the higher of ₹1 lakh or 5% of total donations (Section 115BBC).
  3. Form 10BD: statement of donations, by 31 May; ₹200 a day late fee (Section 234G).
  4. Form 10BE: certificate to each donor by 31 May, so they can claim 80G.
Cash donations above ₹2,000

Donors can’t claim 80G on cash donations above ₹2,000. Encourage UPI or bank transfers and collect donor PAN for Form 10BD.

File on time to keep the exemption (Section 12A)

The exemption under Sections 11 and 12 depends on the audit report being filed and the return being filed within the time allowed. A missed return can make the whole year’s income taxable.

Watch out

If your trust misses a deadline

  • Exemption at risk: a late or missing return or audit report can deny the Section 11 exemption for the year.
  • Interest: 1% a month or part on any tax unpaid from the due date (Section 234A).
  • Form 10BD: ₹200 a day late fee, plus a penalty of ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh.
  • Lapsed registration: without timely renewal, donors lose 80G and the trust can face tax on accreted income (Section 115TD).
  • FCRA: a missed FC-4 can lead to penalties or cancellation of FCRA registration.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

Which ITR form does a trust or NGO file?

Trusts, societies and Section 8 companies registered under Section 12AB or approved under Section 10(23C) that claim exemption file ITR-7. Political parties, research associations, universities and similar institutions also file ITR-7. A trust that is not registered and does not claim exemption files ITR-5 as an association of persons.

Is it mandatory for a registered trust or NGO to file an income tax return?

Yes. Under Section 139(4A), a trust or institution must file its return if its total income, before claiming the exemption under Sections 11 and 12, exceeds the basic exemption limit. Filing on time is also a condition for keeping the exemption.

What is the due date for ITR-7 for FY 2025-26?

Trusts whose accounts must be audited file by the audit-case due date, which for FY 2025-26 is 21 November 2026, extended from 31 October by CBDT Circular 7/2026. Trusts that do not need an audit file by the general due date.

How much of its income must a trust spend?

A charitable or religious trust must apply at least 85% of its income to its objects during the year. Up to 15% can be set aside without conditions. Any further shortfall can be accumulated for up to five years by filing Form 10, provided the money is invested in the modes allowed by Section 11(5).

What happens if a trust does not apply 85% of its income?

The unspent amount that is neither within the 15% allowance nor accumulated through Form 10 or deemed applied through Form 9A becomes taxable at the maximum marginal rate. Accumulated amounts not used within five years also become taxable.

Which audit report does a trust need, Form 10B or Form 10BB?

Form 10B applies if total income before exemption exceeds ₹5 crore, the trust received foreign contribution, or applied income outside India. Other trusts that need an audit use Form 10BB. The audit report must be filed before the return, at least one month before the ITR due date.

Are corpus donations taxable for a trust?

Voluntary contributions made with a specific direction that they will form part of the corpus are exempt and are not counted as income for the 85% rule, but they must be invested in the modes allowed by Section 11(5). Spending from the corpus is not treated as application of income until it is reinvested.

How are anonymous donations taxed?

Under Section 115BBC, anonymous donations above the higher of ₹1 lakh or 5% of total donations are taxed at 30%. Wholly religious trusts are excluded, and partly religious trusts are excluded except for donations made for an educational or medical institution they run.

What is Form 10BD and Form 10BE?

A trust approved under Section 80G or 35 files Form 10BD, a statement of donations received, by 31 May after the year ends, and issues Form 10BE certificates to donors by the same date. Late filing attracts a fee of ₹200 a day under Section 234G and a penalty under Section 271K.

How long is 12AB registration valid?

Regular registration under Section 12AB is valid for five years. From 1 April 2025, it is granted for ten years where the trust's total income, before the exemption, did not exceed ₹5 crore in each of the two years before the application. Renewal must be applied for in Form 10AB at least six months before expiry.

Does an NGO with foreign contribution have extra filings?

Yes. An NGO registered under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act files its annual return in Form FC-4 by 31 December, and its income tax audit must be in Form 10B, whatever its income.

Does the Income-tax Act, 2025 change ITR filing for trusts for FY 2025-26?

Returns for FY 2025-26 are filed under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27 and brings the rules for charitable trusts and institutions together in a single chapter for registered non-profit organisations, so plan next year's compliance with the new references.
