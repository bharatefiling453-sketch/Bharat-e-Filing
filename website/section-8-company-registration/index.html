Overview

What is a Section 8 company?

A Section 8 company is a non-profit company licensed under section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 to promote objects such as education, charity, sports, art, science, social welfare or the environment. It applies all income to those objects, pays no dividends, has no minimum capital and need not use “Limited” in its name. It is incorporated through SPICe+.

The non-profit structure donors, CSR teams and grant makers know best.

Compared with a trust or society, it is registered centrally with the MCA, has clear rules for directors and members, and files its accounts publicly every year. In return, changes to its memorandum and articles need government approval.

How to register

How to register a Section 8 company

  1. DSCs: Class 3 digital signatures for the directors.
  2. Name: reflecting the objects, reserved through SPICe+.
  3. SPICe+ with the licence: MoA and AoA in the Section 8 format, declarations and an income and expenditure estimate.
  4. Licence and certificate: with the CIN, PAN and TAN.
  5. Start up: auditor, bank account, and income tax registration.

The licence comes with conditions

Income must be used only for the company’s objects, with no dividends to members. If the conditions are broken, the Central Government can revoke the licence, and the company and its officers can face heavy penalties.

Compliance

Yearly compliance for a Section 8 company

  1. Board meetings: at least one in every six calendar months.
  2. AGM: by 30 September, or within nine months of the first year end.
  3. AOC-4: within 30 days of the AGM.
  4. MGT-7: within 60 days of the AGM.
  5. Income tax: return every year, with audit and TDS as applicable.

Funding

Getting ready to receive funds

  • Donations: income tax registration, plus donation approval so donors can claim a deduction.
  • CSR funds: registration with the MCA in Form CSR-1.
  • Foreign funds: registration or prior permission under the FCRA, 2010.
  • Government grants: often need a NITI Aayog NGO Darpan ID.
Tax

How a Section 8 company is taxed

A Section 8 company is not tax-exempt by default. Its income is exempt only once it is registered with the Income Tax Department and it meets the conditions. From tax year 2026-27, registration and exemption are under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which treats it as a registered non-profit organisation.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is a Section 8 company?

A Section 8 company is a company licensed under section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013 to promote objects such as commerce, art, science, sports, education, research, social welfare, religion, charity or protection of the environment. It must apply its profits and income only to these objects and cannot pay dividends to its members.

How many members and directors are needed?

A Section 8 company formed as a private company needs at least two members and two directors. One formed as a public company needs at least seven members and three directors. At least one director must have stayed in India for 182 days or more in the financial year.

Is there a minimum capital requirement?

No. A Section 8 company has no minimum capital requirement, and it can also be formed without share capital as a company limited by guarantee.

Does the name need to end with Limited?

No. The licence allows a Section 8 company to drop the word Limited or Private Limited. Names usually include words such as Foundation, Forum, Association, Federation, Council or Institute.

How is a Section 8 company incorporated?

Online on the MCA portal through SPICe+. The licence under section 8 is applied for in the same filing, with the memorandum and articles in the prescribed format and declarations from the subscribers and a professional. The licence is issued along with the certificate of incorporation.

Can members or directors be paid?

Members cannot receive dividends or any share of the profits. The company can pay reasonable remuneration to members, directors or others for services actually rendered, within the rules.

Can a One Person Company be a Section 8 company?

No. A One Person Company cannot be incorporated as, or converted into, a Section 8 company.

What are the yearly compliances?

Board meetings, an annual general meeting, audit of the accounts, financial statements in AOC-4 and the annual return in MGT-7, director KYC, and the income tax return. A Section 8 company needs to hold at least one board meeting in every six calendar months.

Is a Section 8 company exempt from income tax?

Not automatically. It must register with the Income Tax Department as a charitable organisation. For FY 2025-26 this is registration under section 12A or 12AB of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From tax year 2026-27 registration is under the Income-tax Act, 2025, as a registered non-profit organisation.

Can donors get a tax deduction?

Only if the Section 8 company has approval for donations, known as 80G approval under the 1961 Act, with equivalent approval under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Donors then claim the deduction allowed by law.

Can a Section 8 company receive CSR or foreign funds?

To run CSR projects for companies, it registers with the MCA in Form CSR-1. To receive foreign contributions, it needs registration or prior permission under the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, 2010.

What if the company breaks the Section 8 conditions?

The Central Government can revoke the licence, and the company and its officers in default can face heavy fines, and officers possible imprisonment. Changing the memorandum or articles, or converting into another kind of company, needs government approval.
