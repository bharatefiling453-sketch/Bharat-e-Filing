Overview

What is TAN?

TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) is a 10-character number, like DELA12345B, that every person deducting or collecting tax at source must hold under Section 203A. You apply online in Form 49B, and quote the TAN on every TDS challan, quarterly statement and certificate. It is valid for life.

Without a TAN, banks won’t accept your TDS payments, your returns won’t be processed, and your payees won’t get credit for tax you deducted.

The first three letters show the city, the fourth is the first letter of the deductor’s name, followed by five digits and a check letter. TAN is different from PAN, which identifies a taxpayer.

Example · Startup

A Pune software startup hires its first five employees and pays a freelance designer. It needs a TAN to deduct TDS on salary and on the designer’s fees, deposit it by the 7th of the next month and file quarterly statements.

Rekha, a salaried buyer in Chennai, buys a flat for ₹82 lakh. She deducts 1% TDS and pays it with her own PAN in Form 26QB. She does not need a TAN.

One TAN only (Section 203A)

A deductor should hold one TAN. If you already have one, use it, and apply for a correction if details changed. Holding more than one TAN for the same deductor is not allowed; surrender the extra.

Watch out

Penalties every TAN holder should know

  1. No TAN or wrong TAN: penalty of ₹10,000 (Section 272BB).
  2. Not deducting TDS: interest at 1% a month, and the expense may be disallowed.
  3. Late deposit: interest at 1.5% a month or part from deduction to deposit.
  4. Late statement: ₹200 a day, up to the TDS amount (Section 234E).
  5. Very late or wrong statement: penalty of ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh (Section 271H).

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is TAN?

TAN, the Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number, is a 10-character alphanumeric number issued by the Income Tax Department to every person who deducts or collects tax at source. It must be quoted on TDS and TCS payments, quarterly statements and the certificates given to payees.

Who needs to apply for TAN?

Every person responsible for deducting or collecting tax at source must obtain a TAN under Section 203A. This includes companies, firms, LLPs, trusts, government offices, and individuals or HUFs whose accounts are audited, whenever they pay salary, contractor or professional fees, rent, interest, commission or other payments covered by TDS or TCS.

Do individuals need TAN to deduct TDS on property or rent?

No. Individuals and HUFs not required to get a tax audit can deduct TDS on buying property (Section 194-IA), rent above ₹50,000 a month (Section 194-IB) and payments to contractors or professionals (Section 194M) using their PAN, through challan-cum-statements in Form 26QB, 26QC or 26QD. They do not need a TAN for these.

How do I apply for TAN online?

Apply in Form 49B online through the Protean (NSDL) TIN portal. Fill in the deductor category, name, address and contact details, submit, and sign the acknowledgement with Aadhaar e-sign or DSC, or send the signed acknowledgement as instructed. The TAN is then allotted and communicated by email and post.

What documents are needed for TAN registration?

No documents need to be uploaded with an online Form 49B. You need the correct legal name, address of the office that will deduct tax, PAN of the entity where applicable, the deductor category, and the details of the person responsible for deducting tax.

How long does it take to get a TAN?

TAN is usually allotted within about a week to ten working days after the signed application is received. You can track the status on the Protean portal using the 14-digit acknowledgement number.

What is the penalty for not obtaining a TAN?

Failing to apply for a TAN or to quote it where required can attract a penalty of ₹10,000 under Section 272BB. TDS challans and returns without a valid TAN are also not accepted by the banks and the TDS system.

Is TAN valid for life?

Yes. TAN is a lifetime number and does not need renewal. If your name, address or other details change, apply using the form for changes or correction in TAN data. Do not apply for a new TAN for the same deductor, as holding more than one is not allowed.

Can a company have different TANs for its branches?

Yes. Branches or divisions of a company or other entity can obtain separate TANs if each branch deducts tax and files its own TDS statements. Each branch then deposits TDS and files returns under its own TAN.

What do I do after getting a TAN?

Register the TAN on the income tax e-filing portal and TRACES, deposit TDS by the 7th of the next month (30 April for March), file quarterly TDS statements by 31 July, 31 October, 31 January and 31 May, and issue TDS certificates such as Form 16 and Form 16A to payees.

What are the penalties for late TDS deposit or returns?

Interest is charged at 1% a month for not deducting TDS and 1.5% a month for deducting but depositing late. A late TDS statement attracts a fee of ₹200 a day under Section 234E, capped at the TDS amount, and a penalty of ₹10,000 to ₹1 lakh can apply under Section 271H.

Does the Income-tax Act, 2025 change TAN?

No. Your existing TAN continues. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs TDS and TCS for tax year 2026-27, with new section numbers, so check the current challan and statement formats on the e-filing portal and TRACES.
