Overview

How do you increase a company’s authorised capital?

If the articles allow it, the shareholders pass an ordinary resolution under Section 61 to alter the capital clause of the memorandum, and the company files SH-7 with the Registrar within 30 days. Shares can then be allotted up to the new limit, with PAS-3 filed within 15 days of each allotment.

Authorised capital is the ceiling on the shares your company can issue. Raise it before the round, not after.

It is governed by Sections 61 and 64 of the Companies Act, 2013, with allotments under Sections 39, 42 and 62.

When

When companies need a higher limit

  • Funding round through a private placement or preferential allotment.
  • Rights issue to existing shareholders.
  • Bonus issue from reserves, which also needs room in authorised capital.
  • ESOP pool for employees.
  • Loans converted into equity.
Good to know

Authorised vs paid-up capital

Three numbers, often mixed up.

  1. Authorised

    The ceiling

    The most share capital the company may issue, set in the memorandum.

  2. Issued

    Shares allotted Within the ceiling

    The part of authorised capital actually offered and allotted.

  3. Paid-up

    Money received On the balance sheet

    The amount shareholders have paid on the shares allotted.

Increase first, allot second (Section 61)

A company can’t allot shares beyond its authorised capital. Pass the resolution and file SH-7 before the allotment, and check the articles give the power; if not, alter them by special resolution in the same meeting.

Split & consolidate

Other changes to share capital

  • Sub-division: shares split into smaller face value, for example ₹10 into ten ₹1 shares.
  • Consolidation: shares combined into a larger face value.
  • Conversion into stock of fully paid shares, and back.
  • Cancellation of shares not taken up, which reduces authorised capital.

Each is passed under Section 61 and reported in SH-7 within 30 days.

Allotment

After the increase: issuing the shares

  1. Rights issue to existing shareholders under Section 62, or private placement under Section 42.
  2. Money received in a separate bank account for a private placement.
  3. Allotment by board resolution, within 60 days of receiving private placement money.
  4. PAS-3 within 15 days of the allotment, and share certificates issued.

Changing the articles at the same time for investor rights? See AOA Amendment.

Tax records

Capital increase and your tax records

Raising authorised capital creates no tax by itself, and the company keeps its PAN and GSTIN. Shares issued afterwards are reported in the company’s return under the Income-tax Act, 2025, from tax year 2026-27, so keep allotment records and any valuation report ready.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is authorised capital?

Authorised capital is the maximum share capital a company can issue, as stated in the capital clause of its memorandum. Paid-up capital is the part actually issued and paid for. A company cannot allot shares beyond its authorised capital.

How do I increase the authorised capital of a company?

Check that the articles allow it, hold a board meeting to approve and call a general meeting, pass an ordinary resolution under Section 61 of the Companies Act, 2013 altering the capital clause, and file SH-7 with the Registrar within 30 days with the altered memorandum.

Is a special resolution needed to increase authorised capital?

No, an ordinary resolution is enough if the articles authorise the increase. If they do not, the articles are first altered by special resolution, which is also filed in MGT-14.

What is the due date for SH-7?

SH-7 must be filed within 30 days of the resolution altering the share capital, under Section 64 of the Companies Act.

When do companies need to increase authorised capital?

Before issuing new shares beyond the current limit, such as for a funding round, a rights issue, a bonus issue, an employee stock option pool, or converting loans into equity.

Does increasing authorised capital mean issuing shares?

No. It only raises the ceiling. Shares are issued separately, by allotment through a rights issue, private placement, bonus issue or other route, with PAS-3 filed within 15 days of allotment.

What documents are needed for SH-7?

The notice of the general meeting, the ordinary resolution, the altered memorandum, and the altered articles if they were changed, signed with a director's digital signature.

Can a one person company increase its authorised capital?

Yes. In a one person company, the resolution is passed by the sole member and entered in the minute book, and SH-7 is filed within 30 days.

What other changes to share capital use SH-7?

Consolidating shares into larger amounts, sub-dividing shares into smaller amounts, converting fully paid shares into stock and back, and cancelling shares that have not been taken up are all altered under Section 61 and reported in SH-7.

How much should I increase the authorised capital by?

Enough to cover the paid-up capital after the planned issue, with some headroom for the next round or an ESOP pool, so that you do not need to repeat the process soon. Our calculator suggests a figure.

Can authorised capital be reduced?

Unissued shares can be cancelled under Section 61, which reduces authorised capital and is reported in SH-7. Reducing issued or paid-up capital is a different process under Section 66.

Does an increase in authorised capital affect income tax?

No tax arises on increasing authorised capital itself. Share issues that follow are reported in the company's income tax return under the Income-tax Act, 2025 from tax year 2026-27, so keep allotment records ready.
