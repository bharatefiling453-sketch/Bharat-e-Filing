Overview

What is OPC compliance?

OPC compliance is the set of yearly filings a One Person Company must make: audited accounts in AOC-4 within 180 days of the year end, the annual return in MGT-7A, and ITR-6. An OPC needs no AGM, fewer board meetings and pays lower penalties, but late forms still cost ₹100 a day each.

An OPC gives a solo founder limited liability and a company’s credibility, with lighter compliance than a private limited.

OPCs are governed by the Companies Act, 2013, with specific relaxations in Sections 96, 122, 137 and 173, and lower penalties under Section 446B.

OPC relief

What an OPC doesn’t have to do

  • No AGM: exempt under Section 96; resolutions go in the minute book (Section 122).
  • Fewer board meetings: two a year, and none if there is only one director.
  • No cash flow statement: not required in the financial statements.
  • Simpler signing: financial statements signed by one director.
  • Lower penalties: half the normal penalty under Section 446B.
Example · Solo consultant

Kavya runs a data analytics OPC in Kochi as its only director. She holds no AGM and no board meetings. Her auditor signs off in August, she files AOC-4 by 27 September, ITR-6 by 21 November and MGT-7A by 29 November.

Lower penalties, same fees (Section 446B)

OPCs pay half the normal penalty for many defaults, up to ₹2 lakh for the company. But the ₹100 a day late fee on each form applies in full, with no upper limit.

Nominee and changes

Nominee, conversion and other changes

  • Nominee: becomes the member if the sole member dies or can’t act; consent in INC-3.
  • Change of nominee: INC-4 within 30 days, with the new nominee’s consent.
  • Conversion: to a private or public company at any time in INC-6, with at least two members and two directors.
  • New director: DIR-12 within 30 days of appointment.
  • Registered office moves: INC-22 within 30 days.

Filing the OPC’s tax return? See Company ITR Filing. Growing into a private limited? See Company Compliance.

Watch out

What non-compliance costs

  1. Late fees: ₹100 a day for each late AOC-4 and MGT-7A.
  2. Penalties: halved for OPCs, but still payable by the company and the director.
  3. Director disqualification: after three years of missed filings (Section 164).
  4. Strike-off: after two years without filing (Section 248).
  5. Income tax: late ITR-6 means a late fee, interest and lost carry-forward of losses.
New law

OPC compliance and the Income-tax Act, 2025

ROC compliance under the Companies Act is unchanged. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies to tax year 2026-27, so the OPC’s tax filings from then follow new references.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is annual compliance for a One Person Company?

A One Person Company must get its accounts audited, file its financial statements in AOC-4, file its annual return in MGT-7A, file its income tax return in ITR-6, and keep its director KYC, deposits and MSME returns up to date. It does not need to hold an annual general meeting.

What is the due date for AOC-4 for an OPC?

An OPC files AOC-4 within 180 days of the end of the financial year. For FY 2025-26, that is 27 September 2026. A late AOC-4 attracts an additional fee of ₹100 a day with no upper limit.

What is the due date for MGT-7A for an OPC?

An OPC files its annual return in MGT-7A within 60 days of the date on which its AGM would have been due, that is by 29 November 2026 for FY 2025-26. Late filing costs ₹100 a day.

Does an OPC need to hold an AGM?

No. Under Section 96 of the Companies Act, a One Person Company is exempt from holding an annual general meeting. Resolutions that would be passed at a meeting are recorded in the minute book and signed by the member.

How many board meetings must an OPC hold?

An OPC must hold at least one board meeting in each half of the calendar year, with at least 90 days between them. If the OPC has only one director, no board meeting is required; decisions are recorded in the minute book instead.

Is an audit compulsory for an OPC?

Yes. Every company, including an OPC, must have its accounts audited by a chartered accountant. The first auditor is appointed by the board within 30 days of incorporation, and Form ADT-1 is filed within 15 days of an appointment.

What is the nominee in an OPC and how is it changed?

Every OPC names a nominee who becomes the member if the sole member dies or is incapacitated. To change the nominee, the member gives notice and the company files Form INC-4 within 30 days, along with the new nominee's consent in Form INC-3.

Can an OPC convert into a private limited company?

Yes. Since 1 April 2021, an OPC can convert into a private or public company at any time by filing Form INC-6, as there is no longer a capital or turnover limit forcing conversion. It must then have at least two members and two directors.

Who can form an OPC?

Any natural person who is an Indian citizen, whether resident or non-resident in India, can form an OPC and act as its nominee. A resident is someone who has stayed in India for at least 120 days in the previous financial year.

Are penalties lower for OPCs?

Yes. Under Section 446B, an OPC or small company that defaults on certain provisions pays half the normal penalty, up to ₹2 lakh for the company and ₹1 lakh for each officer in default. Late filing fees of ₹100 a day still apply in full.

What tax compliance does an OPC have?

An OPC is taxed as a company. It files ITR-6, pays advance tax, deducts and deposits TDS, and files GST returns if registered. For FY 2025-26, ITR-6 is due by 21 November 2026 under CBDT Circular 7/2026.

Does the Income-tax Act, 2025 change OPC compliance?

ROC compliance under the Companies Act is unchanged. For income tax, the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27, so the OPC's tax filings for that year onwards follow the new section numbers.
