Overview

What is GSTR-10?

GSTR-10 is the final return filed once your GST registration is cancelled. It reports the stock and capital goods you held on the day before cancellation and the tax on them. It is due within 3 months of the cancellation date or order, whichever is later, and a late fee of ₹200 a day applies after that.

Cancelling a GSTIN does not end your GST obligations. Until GSTR-10 is filed, the officer can issue notices and assess tax on the stock you held.

It is governed by Section 45 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 81 of the CGST Rules. The tax on stock is worked out under Section 29(5), the late fee under Section 47, and non-filing is handled under Sections 46 and 62.

Eligibility

Who must file GSTR-10?

Every normal taxpayer whose registration is cancelled must file it, whether the cancellation was:

  • Applied for by you in REG-16, for example on closing the business
  • Ordered by the officer, for example for not filing returns
  • Due to a change in constitution or transfer of the business that needs a new PAN
  • On death of the proprietor, filed by the legal heir
Who does not file GSTR-10

Composition taxpayers, input service distributors, non-resident taxable persons, and persons deducting tax at source (TDS) or collecting tax at source (TCS).

Example · Nil stock

Priya closed her boutique in Jaipur and surrendered her GSTIN, which was cancelled with effect from 2 October 2026. She sold all her stock before closing, so she files a nil GSTR-10 by 2 January 2027 and pays nothing.

Example · Stock and capital goods

A Surat textile trader had ₹1.4 lakh of fabric (credit taken ₹7,000) and a machine bought 7 quarters earlier with ₹18,000 credit. On the fabric he pays the higher of ₹7,000 and the output tax at today’s value. On the machine the credit is reduced by 35% to ₹11,700, compared with the tax on its sale value.

What’s new

Latest changes that affect GSTR-10

Updates that matter for final returns, newest first.

  1. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 in force

    Closing stock in GSTR-10 should match the closing stock in your books and your income-tax return for the year of closure.

  2. October 2025

    3-year limit on returns Act now

    GSTN now blocks GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-9 filed more than three years after their due date. Clear pending returns up to cancellation before they cross that line.

  3. 2023

    Section 62 window extended Law change

    A best judgement assessment is withdrawn if the return is filed within 60 days, extendable by another 60 days with an extra late fee of ₹100 a day.

Pay the higher amount (Section 29(5))

For stock, pay the higher of the credit taken on it and the output tax on its value. For capital goods, reduce the credit by 5 percentage points a quarter since the invoice date and compare it with the tax on the transaction value.

Watch out

If GSTR-10 is not filed

  • Late fee: ₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST a day from the due date, capped at ₹10,000 in all (Section 47).
  • Notice: the officer issues GSTR-3A asking you to file within 15 days (Section 46).
  • Assessment: if you still don’t file, the officer assesses the tax to the best of their judgement (Section 62).
  • Withdrawal: filing a valid GSTR-10 within 60 days of the order withdraws it, extendable by 60 more days with ₹100 a day extra.
  • Recovery: unpaid demands can be recovered from bank accounts and other assets under Section 79.

On the portal

How GSTR-10 is filed on the GST portal

  1. Log in with the cancelled GSTIN’s credentials; access stays open for filing.
  2. Go to Services, Returns, Final Return (GSTR-10).
  3. Enter stock details by HSN, with value, tax rate and credit, under inputs, semi-finished, finished and capital goods.
  4. Pay the tax, interest and late fee from the cash ledger or credit ledger as allowed.
  5. File with DSC or EVC and download the ARN.
Income tax

GSTR-10 and the Income-tax Act, 2025

GSTR-10 is a GST return and is unchanged. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and the closing stock you report in GSTR-10 should match the closing stock in your books and income-tax return.
