Overview

What is income tax e-filing?

Income tax e-filing is filing your income tax return (ITR) online on incometax.gov.in. You report the year’s income, deductions and taxes paid, upload the return and e-verify it within 30 days. For FY 2025-26, the due date for most individuals was 31 July 2026, and belated returns are open until 31 December 2026.

Filing on time keeps your refund moving, lets you carry losses forward and gives you proof of income for loans, visas and tenders.

Returns for FY 2025-26 are filed under Section 139 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, to income from tax year 2026-27 onwards.

Eligibility

Who must file an income tax return?

You must file if your total income before deductions is above the basic exemption limit:

  • ₹4 lakh under the new regime
  • ₹2.5 lakh under the old regime (₹3 lakh at 60 or above, ₹5 lakh at 80 or above)

You must also file, whatever your income, if during the year you:

  • Deposited ₹1 crore or more in current accounts, or ₹50 lakh or more in savings accounts
  • Spent over ₹2 lakh on foreign travel or over ₹1 lakh on electricity
  • Had TDS and TCS of ₹25,000 or more (₹50,000 for senior citizens)
  • Had business turnover above ₹60 lakh or professional receipts above ₹10 lakh
  • Held foreign assets or signing authority in a foreign account, as a resident
Below the limit? Filing still helps

File to claim a refund of TDS, carry forward losses, or show proof of income for a loan or visa.

Example · Salaried

Ritika, a software tester in Pune, earned ₹12.5 lakh in salary. After the ₹75,000 standard deduction her taxable income is ₹11.75 lakh, so the Section 87A rebate brings her tax to nil under the new regime. Her employer deducted TDS, so she files ITR-1 to claim it back as a refund.

Example · Investor

Mohan, a retired teacher in Lucknow, sold equity mutual funds with a long-term gain of ₹1.1 lakh. It is within the ₹1.25 lakh exemption, and listed equity gains up to that limit can be reported in ITR-1, so he does not need ITR-2.

E-verify within 30 days

Under CBDT Notification 5/2022, verify within 30 days of uploading. If you verify later, the date of verification is treated as the date of filing, and late fee may apply. An unverified return is treated as not filed.

Watch out

If you missed the due date

  • Belated return: file until 31 December 2026 under Section 139(4), with a late fee of ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if income is up to ₹5 lakh.
  • Interest: 1% a month or part on unpaid tax from the due date (Section 234A).
  • Losses: business and capital losses cannot be carried forward from a belated return.
  • Revised return: correct a filed return until 31 March 2027, with a fee if revised after 31 December 2026.
  • Updated return (ITR-U): up to 48 months from the end of the assessment year, with additional tax of 25% to 70%, not for a lower tax or higher refund.

After filing

What happens after you e-file

  1. Processing: the department processes the return and sends an intimation under Section 143(1).
  2. Refund: credited to your pre-validated bank account, with interest under Section 244A where due.
  3. Adjustments: if a mismatch is found, you get a notice under Section 143(1)(a) and can respond online.
  4. Demand: pay or contest it within the time given in the intimation.
  5. Records: keep your documents for at least six years for any later scrutiny.
New law
