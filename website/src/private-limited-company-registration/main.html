Overview

What is a private limited company?

A private limited company is a company under section 2(68) of the Companies Act, 2013 with up to 200 members, restricted share transfers and limited liability. It needs at least two directors, one resident in India for 182 days, and two shareholders, has no minimum capital, and is incorporated online through SPICe+.

The structure investors, banks and large customers know best.

It can issue shares to raise funding, continues regardless of changes in ownership, and protects founders’ personal assets. In return it has more compliance: audits, board meetings, an AGM and yearly MCA filings.

How to register

How to register a private limited company

  1. DSCs: Class 3 digital signatures for the directors.
  2. Name: ending with “Private Limited”, reserved through SPICe+.
  3. SPICe+: with e-MoA, e-AoA, director consents and office proof.
  4. Certificate of incorporation: with the CIN, PAN and TAN.
  5. Start up: board meeting, auditor, bank account and INC-20A.

Limited liability comes with duties

A private company must hold board meetings and an AGM, have its accounts audited, and file AOC-4 and MGT-7 every year. Companies other than small companies must also keep their shares in demat form.

Compliance

Yearly compliance for a private company

  1. Board meetings: four a year, or two for a small company.
  2. AGM: by 30 September, or within nine months of the first year end.
  3. AOC-4: within 30 days of the AGM.
  4. MGT-7 or MGT-7A: within 60 days of the AGM.
  5. Income tax: company return every year, with TDS as applicable.

Small company

The small company benefits

  • Who: paid-up capital up to ₹10 crore and turnover up to ₹100 crore, not a holding or subsidiary company.
  • Fewer meetings: one board meeting in each half of the year.
  • Shorter return: MGT-7A instead of MGT-7.
  • Demat: not required under rule 9B while small.
Tax

How a private company is taxed

A private company is taxed as a domestic company. It can opt for a concessional rate of 22%, plus surcharge and cess, by giving up certain deductions. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025.
