Overview

What is a sole proprietorship?

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and run by one person. It isn’t a separate legal entity, uses the proprietor’s own PAN, and has no single registration law. It’s set up through registrations in the business name, such as GST, Udyam and Shop Act, which also serve as proof of business for a current account.

It’s the simplest way to start, with the fewest filings.

The trade-off is unlimited liability: the proprietor is personally responsible for the business’s debts. As you grow, you can move to an LLP or a private limited company.

Example · Interior designer

Studio Kanha in Kochi, an interior design proprietorship, expects ₹15 lakh in receipts. That’s below the ₹20 lakh GST threshold for services, so it registers on Udyam and under the Kerala Shop Act, opens a current account with those two proofs, and can choose presumptive taxation at 50% of receipts as a professional.

How to start

How to start a proprietorship, step by step

  1. Choose a name that doesn’t clash with a registered trademark.
  2. Register on Udyam, and under GST and your state’s Shop Act where needed.
  3. Open a current account with two proofs of business.
  4. Set up books, or opt for presumptive taxation if eligible.
  5. Stay compliant: GST returns, advance tax and the yearly income tax return.

You and the business are one

A proprietorship isn’t a separate legal entity. Its income is your income, taxed under your PAN, and its debts are your debts. Keep business and personal money in separate accounts to make tax and records simpler.

Tax

How a proprietorship is taxed

Business income is added to your other income and taxed at individual rates. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025; FY 2025-26 and earlier follow the 1961 Act.

Presumptive tax

Presumptive taxation, simply

  • Business: 8% of turnover, or 6% of digital receipts; turnover up to ₹2 crore, or ₹3 crore if cash is within 5%.
  • Profession: 50% of receipts; up to ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash is within 5%.
  • Advance tax: pay it all by 15 March.
  • Lower profit? Declaring less can bring audit rules into play; check with a CA.

Bank

Opening a current account

Under RBI’s KYC rules, banks ask a proprietary concern for two documents as proof of business, from a list that includes registration certificates such as Udyam, Shop Act and GST, an IEC, and the proprietor’s income tax return showing business income. A bank can accept one in limited cases after verifying the business.
