Overview

What is business ITR filing?

Business ITR filing is filing the income tax return for business or professional income: ITR-3 or ITR-4 for proprietors, ITR-5 for firms and LLPs, and ITR-6 for companies. For FY 2025-26 it is due by 31 August 2026 without a tax audit, and by 21 November 2026 for audit cases.

The return decides how much tax your business pays, whether losses carry forward, and how banks and buyers read your finances.

Returns for FY 2025-26 are filed under Section 139 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. The Income-tax Act, 2025 applies to income from tax year 2026-27 onwards.

Presumptive taxation

Presumptive taxation: less paperwork, no audit

  • Section 44AD (business): turnover up to ₹2 crore, or ₹3 crore if cash receipts are 5% or less. Profit at 8% of turnover, 6% on digital receipts.
  • Section 44ADA (profession): receipts up to ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are 5% or less. Profit at 50%.
  • Section 44AE (transport): up to 10 goods vehicles. ₹1,000 per tonne a month for heavy vehicles, ₹7,500 a month for others.
The five-year lock-in

If you opt out of Section 44AD, you cannot opt back in for the next five years, and a tax audit applies in those years if your income exceeds the basic exemption limit. Choose with a CA.

Example · Trader

Sanjay runs a hardware store in Indore with ₹1.8 crore turnover, 96% received by UPI and bank. Under Section 44AD he declares 6% on digital and 8% on cash receipts, about ₹10.9 lakh, files ITR-4 and needs no audit.

Example · Consultant

Neha, an interior designer in Bengaluru, received ₹68 lakh, all by bank transfer. She can use Section 44ADA up to ₹75 lakh, declare ₹34 lakh as profit and file ITR-4 without books or an audit.

Tax audit

When a tax audit applies

  • Business: turnover above ₹1 crore, or ₹10 crore if cash receipts and payments are each 5% or less.
  • Profession: gross receipts above ₹50 lakh, unless covered by Section 44ADA.
  • Lower profit: profit below the presumptive rate with income above the exemption limit.
  • Opted out: within five years of leaving Section 44AD, with income above the exemption limit.

The audit report (Form 3CA or 3CB with 3CD) is filed before the return. For FY 2025-26 it was due on 21 October 2026. Missing it can attract a penalty of 0.5% of turnover, up to ₹1.5 lakh, under Section 271B.

File on time to keep your losses (Section 80)

Business losses can be carried forward for 8 years and set off against future business income, but only if the return for the loss year is filed by the due date.

Watch out

If your business misses the due date

  • Late fee: up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, or ₹1,000 if income is up to ₹5 lakh.
  • Interest: 1% a month or part on unpaid tax from the due date (Section 234A).
  • Losses: business and capital losses of the year cannot be carried forward.
  • Belated return: allowed until 31 December 2026; after that only an updated return, with additional tax.
  • Audit: a missed or late tax audit can attract a penalty of up to ₹1.5 lakh (Section 271B).

Running on GST too? See GST Return Filing and GST Annual Return (GSTR-9).

Firms and LLPs

Partners: remuneration, interest and TDS

  1. Remuneration limit (Section 40(b)): on the first ₹6 lakh of book profit or a loss, the higher of ₹3 lakh or 90%; 60% on the rest.
  2. Interest on capital: deductible up to 12% a year if the deed allows it.
  3. TDS (Section 194T): 10% on partner payments above ₹20,000 a year, from 1 April 2025.
  4. Partner’s return: remuneration and interest are taxed as business income; the profit share is exempt (Section 10(2A)).
