Overview

What is a business plan?

A business plan explains what a business will do, who its customers are, how it will run, and how it will make money, with 3 to 5 years of projected financials. Banks ask for a version called a project report for loans such as Mudra, PMEGP and term loans, and investors expect a plan with a financial model.

A good plan answers one question: will this business make enough to repay and grow?

Lenders read it to judge repayment, using ratios such as the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR). Investors read it to judge growth and return. Either way, the numbers must follow from realistic assumptions about prices, costs and sales.

Example · Bakery loan

A baker in Jaipur plans a ₹18 lakh unit: ₹12 lakh of ovens and fit-out and ₹6 lakh of working capital, with ₹3 lakh of own money and a ₹15 lakh loan. The report projects five years of sales and costs, shows an average DSCR above 1.5 and break-even in the first year, and goes to the bank with the loan application.

A plan supports your application; it doesn’t guarantee it

Banks and schemes decide on their own norms: credit history, security, own contribution and eligibility. A clear plan with realistic numbers gives your application its best chance. We never inflate figures to make a loan look better.

Loans

Loans and schemes that need a plan

  1. PM Mudra Yojana: Shishu, Kishore, Tarun and Tarun Plus, up to ₹20 lakh.
  2. PMEGP: project cost up to ₹50 lakh for manufacturing and ₹20 lakh for service, with a subsidy of 15% to 35%.
  3. CGTMSE: a credit guarantee that lets eligible micro and small enterprises borrow without collateral.
  4. Bank term loans: for machinery, premises and expansion.
  5. Working capital: often with CMA data.

Starting up? See Udyam Registration, which many MSME schemes ask for.

Avoid these

Common business plan mistakes

  • Sales with no basis: projections that don’t follow from the market or capacity.
  • Missing costs: working capital, GST, insurance and repairs left out.
  • Statements that don’t tie: cash flow that doesn’t match the balance sheet.
  • No repayment check: no DSCR or a schedule longer than the loan.
Tax

Taxes in your projections

Projected profit should allow for income tax, which from tax year 2026-27 is under the Income-tax Act, 2025, and for GST where registration is needed. Your choice of structure changes both tax and compliance costs.
