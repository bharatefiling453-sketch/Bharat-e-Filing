Overview

What is LUT in GST?

A Letter of Undertaking (LUT) is a declaration filed in Form GST RFD-11 that lets a registered exporter ship goods, bill foreign clients or supply SEZ units without paying IGST. It is valid for one financial year, is accepted as soon as the ARN is generated, and must be renewed every year.

Exports and supplies to Special Economic Zones are zero-rated supplies under Section 16 of the Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act, 2017. The LUT is how you make them without tax, and then claim back the GST you paid on inputs.

The procedure sits in Rule 96A of the CGST Rules, 2017, with conditions in Notification 37/2017-Central Tax. Since 1 October 2023, Section 16 of the IGST Act allows the alternative route, exporting on payment of IGST and claiming it back, only for notified goods and persons. For most exporters the LUT, or a bond, is now the standard route.

With an LUT your working capital stays in the business: you don’t pay 18% IGST upfront on an export and wait months for the refund.

Eligibility

Who can file an LUT?

Any GST-registered person making zero-rated supplies can file an LUT, including:

  • Exporters of goods with an Import Export Code (IEC)
  • Exporters of services: IT, design, consulting, freelancers and agencies billing foreign clients
  • Suppliers to SEZ units or developers for authorised operations
  • Merchant exporters and manufacturers, whatever their turnover
Who cannot file an LUT

A person prosecuted for tax evasion of ₹2.5 crore or more under the CGST Act, the IGST Act or any earlier indirect tax law (Notification 37/2017-Central Tax). They can still export under a bond, usually backed by a bank guarantee.

Example · Services

Neha is a UI designer in Pune billing a US client $5,000 a month. Without an LUT she would add 18% IGST (about ₹75,600 a month) and wait for a refund. With an LUT filed for FY 2026-27, she invoices without IGST, as long as the payment arrives in foreign exchange within one year of each invoice.

Example · Goods

Rakesh exports spices from Kochi to Dubai. His invoice of ₹8,40,000 dated 5 October 2026 must be exported by 5 January 2027. If the shipment slips past that date and no extension is granted, he pays ₹1,51,200 IGST with 18% interest within 15 days.

What’s new

Latest LUT and export changes

Every change that affects exporters filing an LUT, newest first.

  1. FY 2026-27

    LUT for FY 2026-27 enabled on the GST portal Now

    GSTN opened RFD-11 filing for FY 2026-27. Select the right year in the “LUT applied for financial year” list; an LUT filed for 2025-26 does not carry over.

  2. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 in force

    Export income is now taxed under the new Act (section mapping below). The LUT itself is unchanged, but GST export turnover should match your ITR.

  3. 22 September 2025

    GST 2.0 rate rationalisation

    Main slabs are now 5% and 18%, plus 40% for specified goods. Check HSN codes and rates on export invoices and in refund statements.

  4. 1 October 2023

    IGST-paid export route restricted Law change

    Section 16 of the IGST Act, as amended by the Finance Act, 2021, limits exports on payment of IGST to notified goods and persons. Everyone else exports under an LUT or bond.

  5. Circular 37/11/2018-GST

    Late LUT can be condoned

    CBIC clarified that a delay in furnishing the LUT may be condoned and the export allowed under LUT on an ex post facto basis, depending on the facts.

Acceptance (CBIC Circular 8/8/2017-GST)

The LUT is deemed accepted as soon as the acknowledgement is issued on the portal. No officer approval is needed, so you can export under it the same day.

Risk

LUT conditions and what happens if you miss them

  • Goods: export within 3 months of the invoice date, or a longer period allowed by the Commissioner (Rule 96A).
  • Services: receive payment in convertible foreign exchange, or in Indian rupees where RBI permits, within 1 year of the invoice.
  • If you miss it: pay the IGST with 18% interest (Section 50) within 15 days after the period ends.
  • If you don’t pay: the LUT facility can be withdrawn, and exports need IGST paid until the dues are cleared.

After filing

What to do after your LUT is filed

  1. Endorse every export invoice with the Rule 46 wording and quote your LUT ARN.
  2. Report exports correctly: in GSTR-1 (table 6A for exports, 6B for SEZ) and as zero-rated supplies in GSTR-3B.
  3. Keep proof: shipping bills for goods; FIRC, e-BRC or bank advice for services.
  4. Claim your refund of unutilised ITC in RFD-01 within 2 years of the relevant date.
  5. Renew before 1 April every year so the next financial year is covered from day one.
Income tax

LUT, exports and the Income-tax Act, 2025

The LUT is a GST procedure and does not change. From 1 April 2026, export income is taxed under the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaces the 1961 Act.
