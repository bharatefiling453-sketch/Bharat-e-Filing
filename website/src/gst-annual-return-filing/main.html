Overview

What is GSTR-9?

GSTR-9 is the annual GST return that summarises a year of sales, purchases, input tax credit and tax paid. For FY 2025-26 it is due on 31 December 2026. It is optional up to ₹2 crore turnover, and above ₹5 crore you also file the reconciliation statement GSTR-9C.

GSTR-9 brings your twelve GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B returns together, so you can correct omissions and show the department one clean picture of the year.

It is filed under Section 44 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 80 of the CGST Rules. GSTR-9 cannot be revised after filing, and the figures are compared later with your books, your income-tax return and your suppliers’ returns, so careful reconciliation matters more than speed.

Who does not file GSTR-9

Composition taxpayers (they file GSTR-4 by 30 June), casual and non-resident taxable persons, Input Service Distributors, persons deducting TDS or collecting TCS under GST, and overseas suppliers of online information and database access services.

Example · Above ₹2 crore

Sanjay runs an electrical goods shop in Indore with ₹3.1 crore turnover in FY 2025-26. GSTR-9 is mandatory, GSTR-9C is not. Reconciling his year, we find ₹42,800 of ITC on two late supplier invoices; he claims it in his October GSTR-3B, before the 30 November deadline, and files GSTR-9 in December.

Example · Above ₹5 crore

A Bengaluru software firm with ₹7.4 crore turnover files GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C. Its audited revenue is ₹6 lakh higher than GSTR-9 because of unbilled revenue at year end. GSTR-9C shows this as a reconciling item, so no extra tax is payable.

What’s new

Latest GSTR-9 changes

Changes that affect GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26, newest first.

  1. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 in force

    Turnover in GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C is still compared with your income-tax return and tax audit report (section mapping below).

  2. October 2025

    3-year limit enforced on the portal Act now

    GSTN advised that returns, including GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C, cannot be filed after three years from their due date (Section 44(2)). FY 2022-23 closes on 31 December 2026.

  3. 22 September 2025

    GST 2.0 rates in the middle of FY 2025-26

    Main slabs became 5% and 18%, plus 40% for specified goods. GSTR-9 for FY 2025-26 must reconcile supplies and HSN data at old and new rates.

  4. 2025

    GSTR-9C completes the annual return Clarified

    CBIC Circular 246/03/2025-GST: where GSTR-9C is required, the annual return is complete only when both GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C are filed.

  5. FY 2024-25 onwards

    GSTR-9 optional up to ₹2 crore

    Notification 15/2025-Central Tax exempts registered persons with turnover up to ₹2 crore from filing GSTR-9 from FY 2024-25 onwards.

After filing

What to do after filing GSTR-9

  1. Download and keep the filed GSTR-9, GSTR-9C and acknowledgement with your reconciliation working papers.
  2. Pay any extra tax identified through DRC-03 with interest, if not already paid.
  3. Keep records for 72 months from the due date of the annual return (Section 36).
  4. Watch for notices: answer any ASMT-10 or mismatch notice within its time limit.
  5. Start next year right: monthly reconciliation makes the next GSTR-9 quick and clean.
Income tax

GSTR-9 and the Income-tax Act, 2025

From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act. GSTR-9 is unchanged, but its turnover is compared with your income-tax return, AIS and tax audit report.
