Overview

What is a public limited company?

A public limited company is a company that is not a private company under section 2(71) of the Companies Act, 2013. It needs at least seven members and three directors, one resident in India for 182 days, has no maximum on members, its shares are freely transferable, and it is incorporated online through SPICe+.

The structure for wide ownership and public funding.

It can invite the public to subscribe to its shares and debentures, and can later list on a stock exchange under SEBI rules. In return it has stricter governance: board composition rules, demat shares, and limits on related party deals and managerial pay.

Example · Agro processing venture

Seven promoters in Nashik incorporate “Sahyadri Agro Foods Limited” in November 2026 with ₹2 crore capital and three directors. As an unlisted public company below the thresholds, it needs no independent directors yet, but it issues its shares in demat form and files PAS-6 every half year.

How to register

How to register a public limited company

  1. DSCs: Class 3 digital signatures for three or more directors.
  2. Name: ending with “Limited”, reserved through SPICe+.
  3. SPICe+: with the MoA, AoA, seven subscribers, director consents and office proof.
  4. Certificate of incorporation: with the CIN, PAN and TAN.
  5. Start up: board meeting, auditor, ISIN, demat allotment and INC-20A.

    Also at turnover ₹300 crore or more.

Shares must be in demat form

Under rule 9A, an unlisted public company issues securities only in demat form and files a PAS-6 reconciliation every half year. Nidhi companies, government companies and wholly owned subsidiaries are exempt.

Compliance

Yearly compliance for a public company

  1. Board meetings: four a year, with no more than 120 days between two.
  2. AGM: by 30 September, or within nine months of the first year end.
  3. AOC-4 and MGT-7: within 30 and 60 days of the AGM.
  4. PAS-6: within 60 days of each half-year end.
  5. Income tax: company return every year, with TDS as applicable.

Governance

Stricter rules for public companies

  • Related party deals: section 188 approvals, without the relaxations private companies get.
  • Managerial pay: overall limit of 11% of net profits under section 197, unless shareholders approve more.
  • Loans to directors: restricted under section 185, again without the private company relaxations.
  • Listed companies: SEBI listing rules on top of the Companies Act.
Tax

How a public company is taxed

A public company is taxed as a domestic company. It can opt for a concessional rate of 22%, plus surcharge and cess, by giving up certain deductions. From tax year 2026-27 this is under the Income-tax Act, 2025.
