Overview

What is GST revocation?

GST revocation restores a registration that the GST officer cancelled, usually for not filing returns. You apply in Form GST REG-21 within 90 days of the cancellation order, after filing all pending returns and paying dues. The officer decides within 30 days, and your old GSTIN becomes active again.

A cancelled GSTIN stops you from charging GST, issuing tax invoices and generating e-way bills, and your buyers lose input tax credit. Revocation is the fastest way back.

It is governed by Section 30 of the CGST Act, 2017 and Rule 23 of the CGST Rules. The officer revokes in REG-22, may issue a show cause notice in REG-23 (you reply in REG-24), and can reject the application in REG-05.

Eligibility

Who can apply for revocation?

You can apply when the proper officer cancelled your registration on their own motion, commonly for:

  • Not filing returns for a continuous period (Section 29(2))
  • Not conducting business from the declared place of business
  • Not starting business within six months of voluntary registration
  • Contravention of the Act or Rules, as stated in the REG-17 notice
Revocation does not apply if you cancelled it yourself

If you applied for cancellation in REG-16 and it was approved, Section 30 does not apply. To resume business you apply for a fresh registration.

Example · Non-filing

Arif runs a mobile repair shop in Bhopal. He missed six months of returns while unwell, and his registration was cancelled by an order served on 2 October 2026. He has until 31 December 2026 to apply. We file all twelve pending returns, pay the tax and late fee, and file REG-21 with his medical records as the reason. The officer revokes the cancellation in REG-22.

Example · Address not verified

A Kolkata trading firm moved premises without amending its registration, and the officer cancelled it after a failed physical verification. We file REG-21 with the new rent agreement and photographs, and amend the address once the registration is restored.

What’s new

Latest changes that affect revocation

Updates that matter for cancelled registrations, newest first.

  1. 1 April 2026

    Income-tax Act, 2025 in force

    Returns filed for the cancelled period should reconcile with the turnover in your income-tax return.

  2. October 2025

    3-year limit on returns Act now

    GSTN enforced the rule that returns cannot be filed more than three years after their due date. An old pending return can now block a revocation, so act early.

  3. 2023

    90 days to apply Law change

    Rule 23 was amended to give 90 days from the cancellation order (earlier 30 days), with a further extension of up to 180 days by the Commissioner on sufficient cause.

The officer must hear you (Rule 23(3))

Before rejecting a revocation application, the officer must issue a show cause notice in REG-23. Reply in REG-24 within 7 working days; the officer then decides within 30 days of your reply.

Watch out

If the 90 days have passed

  • Days 91 to 270: ask the Commissioner, or an officer not below Joint or Additional Commissioner, to extend the time for sufficient cause.
  • Appeal: appeal against the cancellation order in APL-01 within 3 months of the order, extendable by 1 month (Section 107).
  • High Court: in suitable cases, courts have allowed restoration through writ petitions.
  • Fresh registration: the last option, but past dues on the old GSTIN still have to be settled.

While the registration stays cancelled you cannot collect GST or issue tax invoices, and making taxable supplies without registration can attract tax, interest and penalty.

After revocation

What to do after your GSTIN is restored

  1. File the gap-period returns from cancellation to revocation within 30 days of REG-22.
  2. Restart monthly filing of GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B on time.
  3. Update your details through an amendment if the address or contacts changed.
  4. Tell your buyers your GSTIN is active, so they can claim input tax credit again.
  5. Set reminders so returns are never missed again.
Income tax

GST revocation and the Income-tax Act, 2025

Revocation is a GST process and is unchanged. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, and turnover in the returns you file for the gap period is compared with your income-tax return.

FAQs

Questions? Answered.

What is revocation of GST registration cancellation?

Revocation is the process of restoring a GST registration that the proper officer cancelled on their own motion, for example for not filing returns. It is governed by Section 30 of the CGST Act and Rule 23 of the CGST Rules, and you apply on the GST portal in Form GST REG-21.

What is the time limit to apply for GST revocation?

You must apply in Form REG-21 within 90 days of the date of service of the cancellation order. On sufficient cause, the Commissioner or an authorised officer not below the rank of Joint or Additional Commissioner can extend this by up to 180 more days.

Can I apply for revocation if I cancelled my registration voluntarily?

No. Revocation under Section 30 applies only when the officer cancelled the registration on their own motion. If you surrendered it yourself, you need to apply for a fresh registration.

Do I need to file pending returns before applying for revocation?

Yes. If your registration was cancelled for not filing returns, you must file all pending returns and pay the tax, interest, late fee and any penalty before applying. The application is not entertained while those returns are outstanding.

How long does the officer take to decide a revocation application?

If satisfied, the officer revokes the cancellation in Form REG-22 within 30 days of receiving the application. If the officer plans to reject it, they first issue a show cause notice in REG-23, you reply in REG-24 within 7 working days, and the officer decides within 30 days of your reply.

What do I do after my registration is restored?

Under Rule 23, file all returns for the period from the date of cancellation to the date of the revocation order within 30 days of the revocation order. Then resume your regular GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filing.

What if the 90-day revocation period has passed?

Within the next 180 days, you can ask the Commissioner to extend the time for sufficient cause. After that, the options are an appeal against the cancellation order in APL-01 within 3 months of the order (extendable by 1 month), a writ petition before the High Court in suitable cases, or a fresh registration.

Can old pending returns block revocation?

They can. Since the 3-year time limit was enforced on the GST portal, a return cannot be filed more than three years after its due date. If a pending return is already time-barred, the usual condition of filing all returns cannot be met, so expert advice is needed on appeal or other remedies.

Can I appeal against cancellation instead of applying for revocation?

Yes. You can appeal against the cancellation order before the appellate authority in Form APL-01 within 3 months of the order under Section 107, extendable by 1 month for sufficient cause. Revocation is usually faster when the reason for cancellation, such as non-filing, can be cured.

What happens to my business while the registration is cancelled?

You cannot collect GST, issue tax invoices or generate e-way bills, your buyers cannot claim input tax credit on your supplies, and continuing to make taxable supplies without registration can attract tax, interest and penalty. Restoring the registration quickly limits this damage.

What documents are needed for GST revocation?

You need the cancellation order (REG-19) and show cause notice (REG-17), proof that pending returns are filed and dues paid, the reason for the default with supporting evidence, and GST portal access with DSC or EVC. Where cancellation was for not operating from the declared address, you also need current address proof.

Does the Income-tax Act, 2025 affect GST revocation?

No. Revocation is a GST process and is unchanged. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act, so keep your turnover consistent across GST returns filed for the gap period and your income-tax return.
